# ADR 0041 — The edge signs the identity it resolved; a worker's delegation is a bearer like any other

**Status:** accepted · 2026-10-09 · for v0.13.2 ([#1784](https://github.com/Vexa-ai/vexa/pull/1784),
[#1783](https://github.com/Vexa-ai/vexa/issues/1783)) · applies P20 and P4 · completes decisions 1
and 2 of [ADR-0037](0037-the-target-architecture-one-edge-domains-over-identity-and-runtime.md)
without changing them

## Context

The gateway resolves a bearer through identity and forwards the request with `X-User-Id` and the
headers beside it (email, scopes, bot limit, workspace memberships, webhook). agent-api and
meeting-api derive everything they scope by from those headers, so the headers should carry proof of
where they came from rather than rely on the network path alone. The opt-in
`VEXA_REQUIRE_GATEWAY_IDENTITY` switch checked for a marker header; this ADR replaces it with a
signature the services verify.

A cloud worker's tools were served by a separate MCP upstream (`AGENT_MCP_URL`) that existed only in
the dogfood deployment. ADR-0037 §2 asks for one MCP server, at the gateway, authenticating every
caller, with the worker as an ordinary client of it.

## Decision

1. **The gateway signs what it stamps.** Every forward carries `X-Vexa-Identity`: HMAC-SHA256 over
   the claims the services read, with an issue time and a 60-second expiry, keyed by
   `VEXA_GATEWAY_IDENTITY_SECRET` (the sealed contract `core/gateway/contracts/gateway-identity.v1`).
2. **The services verify at their door.** agent-api and meeting-api install `IdentityGuard`: a valid
   signature (constant-time, ±30 s skew, at most a 300 s lifetime) rebuilds every `x-user-*` header
   from the claims; an `x-user-*` header without one is a `401`. A request that names nobody passes
   to the routes that need nobody (health, bot and runtime callbacks), and there is no fallback
   subject. The switch is removed.
3. **One other way to name a person: the internal tier.** A service holding `INTERNAL_API_SECRET`
   (flows, agent-api calling meeting-api, the dogfood rig) presents `X-Internal-Secret` and names the
   user it acts for. That is the trust the internal tier already carried everywhere else.
4. **A delegation token is resolved by identity.** `/internal/validate` verifies `vxd_` bearers with
   `VEXA_MCP_DELEGATION_SECRET` and answers with the person, scopes `bot` + `tx`, never an admin role,
   and the dispatch's ceiling (`regime`, `workspaces`, `target`) as `delegation`. Every resolver —
   the gateway and flows — therefore authenticates a worker the same way, through the same rate
   limiter, and none of them holds the delegation key. The `AGENT_MCP_URL` branch is removed: `/mcp`
   has one upstream.
5. **The domain owns the policy on the ceiling.** The ceiling rides the signed identity
   (`x-user-regime`, `x-user-delegation-workspaces`). agent-api refuses a worker dispatched without a
   person the verbs that need one (Connections reads and drafts, a stored credential's use, a
   consent request, the person's timezone, a repository load) and a workspace outside its isolation
   set.
6. **Generated at install.** Both keys are generated by every deploy surface — `mint-dev-env.sh`,
   the chart's Secret (kept across upgrades), the Lite entrypoint — and the three services that
   need the identity key refuse to boot without it.

## Trade-off

**A shared HMAC key makes every verifier a possible signer.** agent-api and meeting-api can mint an
identity, as can anything holding `INTERNAL_API_SECRET` through the internal tier. The boundary this
draws is between processes that hold one of those keys and processes that do not — a bot's browser,
an agent worker, any other pod. Binding a service further (for example the credential broker
accepting an actor only with the gateway's own proof) needs a key a verifier cannot sign with: an
asymmetric signature from the gateway. That is a follow-up, not a reason to keep believing headers.

**The internal tier is broad.** It already was. The chart's default NetworkPolicy narrows who can
reach agent-api and meeting-api at all — runtime-managed pods reach meeting-api for their callbacks
and agent-api not at all, and workers are labelled so the policy can tell them from bots.

## Consequences

- A forged or unsigned identity is refused by agent-api and meeting-api; every legitimate caller has
  a path: the gateway (signed), the internal tier (flows, agent-api, the rig), or no identity at all
  (bot and runtime callbacks, the scheduler, the terminal's internal admin routes).
- A worker files friction through the gateway with its delegation token, as `/agent/friction`.
- The chart ships a default NetworkPolicy for agent-api and meeting-api and deploys the MCP service;
  Lite runs it as a program.
- Delegation revocation (a `jti` denylist) lived in the rig; identity does not hold one yet. A token
  lives at most its TTL (an hour by default).
