- **Added:** a meeting knows **who was in it**. Participants are now a first-class relation on the meeting record — email (nullable), display name, role, provenance (`invite` · `platform` · `inferred`), and join/leave times where the platform reports them. `GET /meetings/{id}` returns `participants[]` alongside a `participants_source`, and `GET /meetings?participant=<email>` filters an account by identity, so "every meeting with anyone at this address" is answerable for the first time. Until now the only identity on a stored meeting was the `speaker` display string inside transcript segments, which walled off every meeting-to-CRM automation a consumer would build.
- **Added:** `PUT /meetings/{id}/participants` attaches an invitation's roster to the record (owner-scoped, replace-per-source so a re-delivered invitation converges instead of accumulating). The invitation lane already captures email · name · `ROLE` · `PARTSTAT` off the `.ics` and had nowhere to put it.
- **Honest by construction:** the roster layer and the speaker layer sit **side by side and are not joined**. There is no resolver, no `speaker_label` column, and no confidence score — unattributed speech stays unattributed, and a consumer that wants a mapping produces one itself with the evidence visible. Likewise, a meeting that never had a roster reports `participants_source: "none"`, which is a different answer from a roster that was captured and was empty.
- **Preserved:** nothing that writes identities today was rewired. A calendar feed's `data.attendees` and the capture lane's observed roster keep their writers and are surfaced read-only through the same read path and the same filter, so rosters captured before this relation existed are not invisible to it.
