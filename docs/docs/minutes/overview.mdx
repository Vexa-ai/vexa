**Minutes** is the browser app where people use Vexa day to day. It has three columns: on the left,
your **chats** (a meeting is a chat too); in the middle, the **conversation** with your agent; on the
right, the **pages** that conversation reads and writes. Everything in Minutes goes through the same
APIs that are documented elsewhere on this site. Minutes adds no logic of its own.

| Read | For |
|---|---|
| [Chats](/minutes/chats) | sending, queueing, background jobs, suggestions, where a chat writes |
| [Pages](/minutes/pages) | the navigator, editing, Extend and Create, images, diagrams, moving pages |
| [Meetings](/minutes/meetings) | live transcript, Highlight, playback, Ask about this, deleting a meeting's data |
| [Workspaces and connections](/minutes/workspaces) | front page, members and invites, links, attaching a repository, your connected accounts |
| [Manage your chats](/how-to/chat-controls) | renaming, ordering, activity and deleting chats |
| [Onboarding research and Extend](/how-to/onboarding-research) | filling your workspace from your accounts |

## What you can do

### Open Minutes and sign in

Open your deployment's terminal address. The sign-in card offers **Continue with Google** or
**Continue with Microsoft** when they are configured, and **Send me a link** to get a one-time sign-in
link by email. Any link you followed (to a meeting, a page or a question) is kept through sign-in.
Who may sign in is set by the operator; see [Authentication](/authentication).

On a new deployment, the first person sees **Set up your instance** and enters the one-time claim code
from the operator's logs to become the administrator. The screen disappears once an administrator
exists.

### The rail

The left column is headed **Chats**.

- By default it shows the chats you wrote in and meetings that are live or upcoming. **All** shows
  everything; the number beside it is how many are hidden.
- **+** (*New chat*) opens a chat that is saved when you send the first message.
- A finished meeting's chat is tagged *held*. A chat's target workspace shows on its row.
- With nothing in it: *Meetings arrive by invitation; "+" starts a chat.*

Activity dots, renaming, drag-to-reorder and deleting are in [Manage your chats](/how-to/chat-controls).

### The context bar

Above the conversation: *Chat ›* or *Meeting ›*, the chat's name, a pill saying what kind of chat it is
(*chat*, *chat · admin*, or *meeting · upcoming*, *live* or *held*), and one chip per workspace in the
chat. The chips choose where the chat writes; see [Chats](/minutes/chats#choose-where-a-chat-writes).

### Fold and resize

- **‹** / **›** fold the chat list (*Hide the chat list*) or the pages panel (*Hide the pages panel*)
  to a thin strip; click the strip to show it again. Your browser remembers the choice.
- Drag the **Resize pages panel** handle, or focus it and press <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>→</kbd> (24 px a
  step). The pages panel is at least 240 px and at most 60% of the window, and never leaves the
  conversation less than 280 px. The width is remembered.

### Your account

The badge at the foot of the rail shows your initials, name and email. Its menu has **Connections**
([your connected accounts](/minutes/workspaces#connect-your-accounts)), **Dark mode** / **Day mode**,
and **Sign out**, which also clears what this browser stored for you.

### When a new version is deployed

A bar appears at the top: *A new version is ready — reload*, with **Reload**. Minutes never reloads by
itself, so nothing you are typing is lost. The check runs every minute and when the tab regains
focus.

### Links into Minutes

| Link | Opens |
|---|---|
| `/w/<workspace>/<path>` | that page (the workspace's front page with no path); see [links](/minutes/workspaces#link-to-a-page) |
| `/?meeting=<platform>/<meeting-id>` | that meeting's room |
| `/?meeting=…&view=transcript` (or `minutes`, `brief`, `file:<path>`) | the room with that tab in front |
| `/?ask=<name>` | a chat that opens with the question the administrator saved under that name |
| `/?s=<id>` | a chat prepared for you, for example on your first sign-in or from a link Vexa sent |
| `/join?i=<token>` | an invite to a workspace; see [Join from an invite](/minutes/workspaces#join-from-an-invite) |

A prepared chat (`?s=`) opens only for the person it was made for. Vexa removes its id from the address
bar once it has been read.

## Limits and errors

| You see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *This link isn't open to you.* · *This link belongs to someone else.* | a prepared chat made for another address, or one that no longer exists | **Switch account**, or ask for a new link |
| *This link is not readable.* | the link is malformed; nothing was opened | ask for a fresh link |
| *We could not reach the service that holds this link.* | the service did not answer; the link is kept | reload in a moment |
| *Your session ended — sign in again.* | your sign-in expired or was revoked | **Sign in again** |
| *That is not this instance's claim code…* · *This instance already has an administrator.* | a wrong claim code, or the instance is already claimed | use the code from the logs, or sign in |

A `?ask=` name must be lowercase letters, digits, `-` or `_` (up to 64 characters). Other names are
ignored.

## How it works

### Flow

1. **Sign-in.** The terminal's `/api/auth/*` routes call admin-api. On success the terminal sets two
   `httpOnly` cookies (`vexa-token`, `vexa-user-info`; `SameSite=Lax`, 30 days). The browser's
   JavaScript cannot read the token.
2. **Every request.** The browser calls only the terminal's own `/api/*`. The terminal server reads
   your token from the cookie and forwards to the gateway (`GATEWAY_URL`) as `X-API-Key`. The gateway
   resolves the key, strips any identity headers the request carried, and forwards to the owning
   service with your identity signed. Paths starting `meetings`, `transcripts`, `bots`, `recordings`
   or `user` go to meeting-api; everything else goes to agent-api under `/agent/*`.
3. **The rail.** `GET /api/sessions` returns your chats from agent-api; the browser merges in chats it
   has not saved yet, and the server's list wins. Names are refreshed every five seconds.
4. **Arrival.** After a first sign-in, the terminal asks agent-api over its internal interface whether
   you have history; if not, it prepares a first chat and lands you on `/?s=<id>`. Opening `?s=` reads
   the prepared chat once, as you.
5. **Version.** `GET /api/version` reports the terminal's build and agent-api's version, and whether
   they are a matching pair. The bar appears when either changes or they stop matching.

### Who writes what

| Data | Single writer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts, sign-in tokens | admin-api | the identity database |
| Your chat list, names, order | agent-api | Redis, per user |
| Prepared chats (`?s=`) | agent-api | Redis |
| Folding, panel width, theme, unsaved chats | your browser | `localStorage` |

### Routes

| Browser calls (terminal) | Goes to |
|---|---|
| `/api/auth/*` (login, request-link, redeem, me, logout, claim-admin) | admin-api |
| `/api/sessions`, `/api/chat/*`, `/api/workspace/*`, `/api/scaffolds/*` | agent-api, through the gateway's `/agent/*` |
| `/api/meetings`, `/api/transcripts/*`, `/api/bots/*`, `/api/recordings/*` | meeting-api, through the gateway |
| `/api/version` | the terminal itself, plus agent-api `/api/version` |

### Configuration

Minutes is a build of the terminal: set `NEXT_PUBLIC_TERMINAL_MODE=minutes` when the image is built
(in Compose, `TERMINAL_MODE=minutes` in `.env`). It is baked in at build time; changing it needs a
rebuild. The value `meetings` builds a meetings-only terminal instead, which refuses every agent path
with `404`. The terminal server reads `GATEWAY_URL` and `AGENT_API_URL`, and refuses to start without a
`NEXTAUTH_SECRET` of at least 32 bytes; see [Configuration](/configuration).

## Why it complies

### Architecture

| Rule | How this design meets it | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P2** contracts · **P6** one front door | the browser calls only the terminal's `/api/*`; the terminal opens only the gateway, plus the agent-api and admin-api doors listed and reasoned in the domain-doors allowlist | `gate:isolation`, `gate:exports`, `gate:domain-doors` |
| **P12** every folder self-documents | each terminal folder, and this docs section, carries a README | `gate:readme` |
| **P18** fail loud | an unreachable gateway is a `502 upstream_unreachable`; a link that cannot be checked says so and keeps the link; the version bar reports a mismatched pair | ungated: reviewed |
| **P20** authorize every access | identity is the gateway-signed one, never a value from the browser; a prepared chat is readable only by its recipient | `gate:access` and the deny tests below |
| **P23** one writer per carrier | agent-api writes the chat list and order; admin-api writes accounts; the terminal stores nothing server-side | `gate:dataflow` |

### Security

**Who can do what.** Anyone the operator allows can sign in. You see only your own chats. The first
administrator is set only with the one-time claim code.

**Protections.**

- The sign-in token lives in an `httpOnly` cookie and is never exposed to page scripts. The
  user-info cookie is for display only; the server confirms identity with admin-api.
- The gateway rejects a missing or invalid key and strips identity headers a caller tries to supply.
- A `401` from the identity check ends the session; other refusals do not sign you out.
- Prepared chats are minted only over the internal interface, read only by their recipient, and never
  carry a share token in the address.
- A claim is refused without a valid sign-in, with a wrong code, or once an administrator exists, and
  uses the identity admin-api returns, never one from a cookie.
