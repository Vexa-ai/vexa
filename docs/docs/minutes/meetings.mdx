A meeting in Minutes is a chat bound to that meeting, with the **meeting view** beside it. The view is
the same wherever you open the meeting (the chat, the Calls list, or a `/?meeting=…` link). It has
three parts, top to bottom:

1. **Meeting tools (top, fixed).** The meeting's name — its calendar title or the name you gave it,
   else its chat's name, else the people in it (*Call with Valeriia*), else *Call with Vexa*; never a
   meeting id — with its status (*Live*, *Joining*, *Ended*…), then the platform as an icon, date and
   time, how long it ran, and who was there (initials and names). One row of actions: **Share**,
   **Update notes**, and **…** (Edit notes, Delete, and Stop bot while it runs; rename, export,
   language and *Send bot again*). The recording player sits here and is the one playback control.
2. **The meeting's note (middle, scrolling).** The note file, rendered exactly as a page is: people,
   companies, projects and other meetings it names are chips, and a section with nothing in it is
   not shown. Edit it from **…** → **Edit notes** (the editor shows the words only) or ask the agent;
   a click on the note follows links, it does not open an editor. No file path is shown; the file is
   in Files like any page, where it opens as a plain page with a *Meeting: (its name) · open* chip and
   none of the meeting's tools. A meeting has one note, and every update, chat request and the
   post-meeting report write that one. The note lives in your Personal workspace, or in the workspace your chat
   works in when Personal is switched off; the sidebar finds it by its meeting wherever it is, and a
   report left in a switched-off Personal workspace is shown read-only.
3. **Transcript (bottom drawer).** What was said, from the meeting itself, never copied into the note.
   Collapsed it is the latest line only — a live dot while live, then *Speaker: what was said* — or
   *Listening…* before anyone has spoken. Opened, its header shows *Transcript*, the number of lines,
   search and copy.

A live meeting's tab is pinned first in the right panel of every chat, marked *Live*, until it ends.
The note file opened on its own (from Files) is a plain page with a *Meeting: (its name) · open* chip at
its top; it does not embed the meeting.

This page covers what you can do with a meeting from the moment the bot is sent.

## What you can do

### Send a bot

In an empty chat, press **Paste a meeting link** (or paste one) and ask the agent to join. When the bot
is sent, the chat binds to that meeting and its pages are pinned. A meeting you start inside a shared
workspace belongs to that workspace, so its members can open it too; a meeting from your personal workspace stays
yours. Meetings also arrive by calendar invitation. See [Send a bot](/how-to/send-a-bot) for platforms
and links.

### A page made before the meeting

An agenda or brief written for a meeting becomes the meeting's page when it is bound to the meeting:
the sidebar shows it, **Update notes** writes it and the post-meeting report lands in it. A meeting has
one page.

- **Before the meeting exists**, the agent's `meeting_attach_page` (with the meeting's link, or its
  native id from `parse_meeting_link`) stamps the page; when the bot joins, the meeting adopts the
  page whose `native`, `meeting_url` or `calendar_event` equals its own. Only an exact match is
  adopted; a title that looks alike is not.
- **After**, on the page's **…** choose **Use as meeting page…** and pick the meeting, or ask the
  agent. If the meeting already has a page with words in it, you are told which; merge, or use this
  page instead on purpose.

The page must be in `kg/entities/meeting/` in one of your workspaces. The binding is recorded on the
meeting with the page's workspace.

### Follow it live

- A state card says what the bot is doing: *Bot at the door*, *Bot needs someone to let it in*,
  *Bot admitted · no words for …*, *Reconnecting to the bot…*, *Bot left*, or *Bot feed error*.
- The transcript panel shows the newest lines, and moves as people talk (*Nothing said yet —
  this fills in as the room talks.*). Opening a meeting part-way through loads its whole history,
  then continues live. The replay keeps lines the bot withdrew out of the view and carries on across
  a resumed session. After a dropped connection, the transcript continues from the last line it received.
- **Stop bot** takes the bot out of the meeting. It is shown while the bot is requested, joining,
  waiting, active or stopping, and only to the meeting's owner.

### Read and update the notes

The notes sit at the top of the view, labelled **Notes**. A line beside the label says how current
they are: *Updated 14:32 · transcript to 12:05* (the time of the
last change to the file, and how far into the meeting the last update read).

- **Update notes** asks the agent, in the meeting's chat, to read what was said since the last update
  and rewrite the notes from it: what the meeting is about, decisions, commitments, people and open
  questions. Each person, company, project or other meeting the notes name is linked to its page;
  a clearly identified person or company with no page gets one, with the meeting as its source, and
  each linked page names the meeting back. A speaker the transcript could not identify stays plain
  text. Text you wrote yourself outside those sections is left alone. The update runs as a
  [background job](/minutes/chats#background-jobs); the button shows its progress, and the notes
  re-render when it is done. An open meeting view checks its note every few seconds, so a change
  written from another chat or by the post-meeting report shows up without a reload.
- Notes change only when you press **Update notes** or ask for a change in the chat (for example
  "add that Ravi owns the pricing follow-up"), and once when the meeting ends: the post-meeting report
  is written into **Report**, and fills **Decisions**, **Commitments** and **Open questions** only
  where those are still empty. A page still named after the platform ("Google Meet meeting") takes
  the meeting's name (the calendar event's, or the one it was given). Sections and titles you wrote
  are never replaced.
- You can also open the file itself from the navigator and edit it; see [Pages](/minutes/pages).

When a meeting has no notes yet, the view is the same with *No notes for this meeting yet* in place of
the notes, and the header's notes button reads **Create notes**. **Create notes** creates the file and, if anything has been
said, fills it from the transcript the same way **Update notes** does. A notes file can contain a
transcript marker (`<!-- vexa:transcript meeting=… -->`, which older versions rendered as a live
transcript inside the page). The view leaves it out when it shows the notes, because the transcript is
right below. The file itself is not changed.

### Search the transcript

Open the drawer and press the search icon. Only the speaker turns that say it (or whose
speaker's name matches) stay, with the words marked; *Nothing matches "…"* when none do. Clear the box
to see everything again. Each turn shows the speaker and the time; click a line to play from there.

### Resize the transcript

Drag the drawer's top edge (or touch and drag on a phone) to show anywhere from the one collapsed line
to nearly the whole sidebar; the note above shrinks to match. It snaps shut near the bottom and to
about a third and two thirds. Double-click the edge to toggle between collapsed and your last height,
which is remembered in this browser.

### Expand the transcript

The transcript sits in its own panel under the notes, with a header that reads **Transcript**, the
number of lines, and *live* while the bot is in. Each line shows the speaker and time on the left and
what was said beside it. The panel starts collapsed, live or finished: a window about four lines
tall, sitting on the newest line, with a fade above when there is more. Scroll inside it (or focus it
and use the arrow keys) to read back through the whole transcript. During a live meeting it stays on
the newest line until you scroll up; then **Jump to latest** takes you back.

Press **Show all** to open the whole transcript in a narrow sidebar on the right of the meeting view,
beside the notes, full height. Drag its left edge (or focus the edge and use <kbd>←</kbd> and
<kbd>→</kbd>) to make it wider or narrower. **Copy transcript** and **Highlight** are in the sidebar.
When the view is too narrow for a sidebar (on a phone, or with the panel squeezed), the transcript
opens as a full-width sheet over the notes instead. **Collapse** puts it back under the notes. Select
text anywhere in the transcript to **Ask about this**.

**Play from a line.** Click a line (or press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> on it) to play the
recording from there. While the recording plays, the line being played is shaded and the panel scrolls
to keep it in view, collapsed or expanded. If you scroll away yourself it stops following, and
**Follow playback** brings it back. Minutes remembers whether you expanded each meeting, and the
sidebar's width, until you close the browser tab.

Sections of the notes that are still empty (for example *Report* before the meeting's report is
written) are not shown.

### Share it

The owner presses **Share** in the meeting's header to invite people by email, add the meeting's
workspace, see who has access and take it back, live or after the meeting. The recording is shared
only if the owner allows it. See [Share a meeting](/how-to/share-a-meeting).

### Highlight

Expand the transcript and press **Highlight**. The agent picks out the people, companies and projects the
meeting has named and marks them on the transcript; nothing is posted to the chat. Pressing it again
adds only what was said since (*Highlight · N* shows how many terms there are).

- A **solid** term has a page: click to open it.
- A **dashed** term has none yet: click (*Find out what … is*) and the chat looks it up.

Highlight works on live and finished meetings.

Pressing Highlight again on a finished meeting whose terms are already marked finds nothing new,
which is correct: the agent's scan says *nothing was said after* the last cursor and how many
segments the transcript holds. A scan of an empty transcript says so instead
(*the transcript has no segments yet*), so the two are never confused.

### Ask about a passage

Select text in the transcript and press **Ask about this** (*Add this quote to your chat*). The quote,
with the meeting it came from, is drafted into the chat box. Nothing is sent until you send it.

To bring the notes up to date with everything said since the last update, use **Update notes**.

### Copy the transcript

**Copy transcript** copies every confirmed line with its speaker; see
[Manage your chats](/how-to/chat-controls#copy-a-meeting-transcript).

### Play the recording

On a finished meeting, the controls load the recording (*Loading recording…*). The player is one row
under the page's title: the play/pause icon, the time (*0:00 / 51:13*), the **Seek recording** slider
filling the rest of the row, and the download icon. On a narrow panel the slider shrinks; the row stays
on one line. There is no mute: use your system volume.

1. Press the play icon in the player row (it turns into
   *Pause*). Nothing plays by itself.
2. Click any transcript passage, or press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> on it, to jump there.
   Hovering a passage shows *Play from m:ss* just above that line. The passage being played is shaded.
3. Drag the **Seek recording** slider to move anywhere in the audio.

Highlighting follows the passage, not each word. Selecting text does not jump the recording, so
**Ask about this** still works, and a highlighted term's link opens as before.

There is one player per audio file, and starting one meeting pauses any other. New recordings carry
the time capture started, so passages line up with the audio. Older recordings have no exact start
mark, so timing uses the meeting's start and may be off by a little (the controls say so).

### Download the audio

Press the download icon at the end of the player row (*Download audio*). The browser saves the
recording as `<platform>-<meeting-code>-<date>.<ext>` (for example `zoom-84512345678-2026-10-09.webm`),
in its own format (usually `.webm`). It goes
through the same owner-only check as playback. If the download is refused or interrupted, the
controls say so under the player (*Could not download audio…*). Over the API, see
[Recordings](/how-to/recordings).

### Delete a meeting's data

On a finished meeting, the owner finds **Delete** in the tools' **…** menu.

1. Press it. The dialog reads *Delete this meeting?*: *Its transcript, notes and recording are deleted
   for good, on every device. Pages saved in a workspace stay.*
2. Type `delete`. **Delete meeting** stays disabled until you do.
3. When it is done: *Meeting audio, transcript and fixtures deleted. Saved pages are kept.*

The meeting stays in your list, marked as deleted, and its transcript says *This meeting's transcript
and recording were deleted.* Pages written from it are not touched.

### Find a call

To find a call by name or date, use **Calls** in the page navigator; see
[Pages](/minutes/pages#find-a-call).

### Rename a call

If you own the call, click its name in the page header, type the new name and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
The list, the chat list and the tab follow it. A call shared with you has no rename control. See
[Pages](/minutes/pages#rename-a-call).

### Open a meeting by link

`/?meeting=<platform>/<meeting-id>` (or a meeting's row number) opens that meeting's view. Add
`&view=file:<path>` to open a page in front of it. Older links with `&view=transcript`, `minutes` or
`brief` still work and open the meeting view. See [Links into Minutes](/minutes/overview#links-into-minutes).

## Limits and errors

| You see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *No audio recording available.* | the meeting has no recording, or you are a workspace member rather than the owner: recordings are served to the meeting's owner only | ask the owner |
| *Could not load recording* · *Audio could not be played. Retry loading the recording.* | the recording could not be fetched or played | press **Retry** |
| *Could not stop bot. Retry.* | the stop request failed | retry; the bot is also ended when the meeting ends |
| *Could not delete meeting data. Retry.* | the delete failed; storage may be unavailable | retry later |
| *No notes for this meeting yet* | nothing has created the meeting's notes on your desk | press **Create notes** |
| *You can't create notes for this meeting.* | the meeting is not yours, not shared with you, and not in a workspace you belong to | ask the owner |
| *Could not load the notes. Retry.* | the notes file could not be read | press **Retry** |
| *Could not download audio: the recording was not found.* · *Could not download audio (HTTP …). Retry.* · *…the connection failed. Retry.* | the download was refused or did not complete | press the download icon again; a recording you do not own is never served |
| *This page declares a transcript, but names no meeting.* | a page opened on its own has a transcript marker without a meeting id | fix the marker or ask the agent |
| *This meeting's transcript and recording were deleted.* | the meeting's data was deleted | — |
| *Bot feed error* · *Unreadable transcript frame* | the live feed failed or sent something unreadable | the card reconnects; dismiss it |

Delete is offered only after a meeting has finished; the server refuses it while the meeting is still
running. Stop and Delete are not shown on a meeting someone shared with you.

## How it works

### Flow

1. **List and open.** The terminal's catch-all proxy sends `/api/meetings` and
   `/api/transcripts/by-id/{id}` to meeting-api through the gateway. The view is bound to the meeting's
   row id; it opens without asking for the notes, so it never depends on the file existing.
2. **Notes.** The view asks agent-api where the notes are (`GET /api/meeting/note?meeting_id=…`).
   agent-api checks that you may read the meeting, then answers with a file on *your* desk, or
   `null`. The terminal reads the file through `/api/workspace/file` and the time of its last change
   through `/api/workspaces/personal/git/history`, and drops any transcript marker before rendering.
   It re-reads the notes whenever a turn commits to the workspace.
3. **Create notes.** `POST /api/meeting/note {meeting_id}`. agent-api checks access, then creates the
   page from its template and commits it. A page that is already there is returned untouched; nothing
   is overwritten.
4. **Update notes.** The terminal sends an Extend act naming the notes file and the meeting
   (`intent: {kind: "extend", path, meeting}`) into the meeting's chat. agent-api checks that you may
   read the meeting, then runs the `extend-meeting` ask as a background job: the agent reads the
   transcript from meeting-api since the page's `transcript_cursor`, rewrites the notes' sections, and
   moves the cursor. The ask names the note's workspace (your personal workspace when the note is
   there), and a write of a meeting's note that names no workspace lands where the note is, never in
   the workspace the chat works in. The same holds for a note the agent writes with its own file
   tools: when the turn commits, that change is merged into the meeting's note and the stray copy is
   removed. Asking for a change in the chat goes through the same agent.
   A new note starts empty: sections appear with their first content.
5. **Live transcript.** The browser opens `/api/meeting/stream?meeting_id=…&session_uid=…` as
   server-sent events. agent-api checks that you may read the meeting, then reads the meeting's
   transcript stream: from the start on a fresh connection, then live. A reconnect resumes from the last
   event it saw.
6. **Playback.** The controls list recordings at `/api/recordings?meeting_id=…` and stream
   `/api/recordings/{id}/media/{media}/raw?type=audio`. The terminal passes byte ranges through, so the
   player can seek; meeting-api serves the ranges from object storage. Download reads the same route
   with `download=1`, which adds `Content-Disposition: attachment`. A transcript line's play position
   is its timestamp minus the recording's capture start.
7. **Highlight.** The button sends a silent `highlight` request into the chat. The agent scans the
   transcript for candidates and publishes the ones it keeps; the terminal reads them from
   `/api/meeting/terms`. Terms are added, never overwritten. The scan's `since` cursor is the last
   segment's time from the previous scan; `shared/terms.py` `segments_since` compares it to each
   segment as a time (ISO-8601 with `Z` or an offset) or as a number (a relative start), so a
   cursor's spelling never hides later segments. Every answer carries `transcript_segments` (the
   whole transcript) and `cursor_note`, which says whether an empty scan means an empty transcript
   or nothing new after the cursor. The agent-api route (`meeting_highlight.scan`) and the dogfood
   rig's `transcript_terms` both call the same two functions.
8. **Delete.** `DELETE /api/meetings/{id}` reaches meeting-api, which deletes the stored objects first,
   then the fixtures, then the transcript rows and the meeting's data keys, and marks the meeting as
   deleted.
9. **Stop.** `DELETE /api/bots/{platform}/{meeting-id}` reaches meeting-api's stop route.

### Who writes what

| Data | Single writer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting rows | meeting-api | Postgres |
| Transcript segments | meeting-api | Postgres, and the meeting's transcript stream in Redis (agent-api and the terminal only read it) |
| Recording master | meeting-api (from the bot's chunks) | object storage |
| Meeting notes | agent-api (the mint, and the agent's Update) | `kg/entities/meeting/<date>-<slug>.md` in your personal workspace's git repository |
| Highlight terms | agent-api | `.vexa/meeting-terms/<meeting>.json` in the reader's personal workspace |

### Routes

| Browser calls (terminal) | Goes to |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/meetings`, `GET /api/transcripts/by-id/{id}` | meeting-api, through the gateway |
| `GET /api/recordings`, `GET /api/recordings/{id}/media/{media}/raw` (with `Range`; `download=1` to save) | meeting-api, through the gateway |
| `DELETE /api/meetings/{id}`, `DELETE /api/bots/{platform}/{id}` | meeting-api, through the gateway |
| `GET /api/meeting/stream` (server-sent events) | agent-api `/api/meeting/stream`, through the gateway's `/agent/*` |
| `GET`, `POST /api/meeting/note`; `GET /api/meeting/terms` | agent-api, through the gateway's `/agent/*` |

The terminal decides the destination by path: `meetings`, `transcripts`, `bots`, `recordings` and `user`
go to the meetings side, everything else to the agent. The meetings routes are documented in the
[Meetings API](/api/meetings).

### Configuration

`GATEWAY_URL` on the terminal server. Recording storage is configured on meeting-api; see
[Recordings](/how-to/recordings).

### Security

**Who can do what.** You can open a meeting you own, a meeting shared with you, and a meeting that
belongs to a workspace you are a member of, and create or update notes for it. Notes are written to
your own desk. Anyone but the owner can play the recording only if the owner allowed it when sharing
([Share a meeting](/how-to/share-a-meeting)). Only the owner can stop the bot, delete the meeting's
data or change who has access.

**Protections.**

- The user is the gateway-signed identity. Workspace membership is read from the workspace's roster,
  never from a request header.
- A meeting you may not read is refused before the live stream opens, and a stream for someone else's
  bot session is refused.
- Someone who is not the owner gets the same `404` as for a meeting that does not exist, so meetings
  cannot be discovered.
- A deleted meeting's transcript is refused, and new segments for it are refused.
- Recording responses are `private, no-store`. Only the owner, or a workspace member the owner shares with, can subscribe to a transcript.
- A download is the playback read with `download=1`: the same owner check, and nothing else changes.
  Its file name is built only from server-side fields (meeting id, recording date, stored format),
  never from the request, and the browser fetches it through the session proxy with no credential in
  the URL.
- Meeting ids in links are checked so they cannot escape their path segment.
