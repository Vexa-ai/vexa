---
title: "Shared chats"
description: "Add people to a chat, how the agent behaves with more than one person in it, and how conversations are stored apart from workspaces."
---

A chat is a conversation. Vexa keeps every conversation in its session store, apart from all of your
workspaces: no workspace, not even your private `_system`, holds a chat, so you can add people to one
without sharing anything else. Adding someone shares the conversation and nothing else:
they do not get your workspaces.

## What you can do

### Add people with @

1. In the message box, type **@** and pick a person. The list holds the people you share a workspace
   with, and nobody else. The message shows `@Name`, and it carries that person's account, so the
   name always means exactly one person.
2. Press **Enter**. If someone you mentioned is not in the chat yet, Vexa asks first, above the
   message box: **Share this chat with Anna?**
   - In a chat only you are in, the question says what Anna will see: this chat, with what was said
     in it and the agent's answers, and which of your private reads (Gmail, Calendar, meetings) that
     history holds.
   - In a chat that already has other people, Anna joins it and sees it, as everyone there does.
3. Choose one:
   - **Share and send** adds them to this chat, history included, then sends the message here.
   - **New chat with them** (a chat only you are in) starts a new shared chat with them that begins
     with this message. This chat and what you said in it stay yours.
   - **Send without sharing** sends the message with the name as plain text. Nobody is told, and the
     agent is not given their account.
   - **Cancel** sends nothing.

Only the chat's owner shares its history. A member who adds someone starts a new chat of their own
with that person, which starts empty.

### See and remove people

The bar above the chat shows who is in it (**Just you**, or **N people**). Press it to see the list.
The owner can press **Remove** beside a person. Anyone except the owner can press **Leave**. The owner
cannot be removed. People are not added here: mention them with **@**.

### Find a chat shared with you

When someone adds you to a chat, it appears in your chat list at once, without a reload, in a section
called **Shared with me**, and a [notification](/minutes/notifications) says who added you. The row
carries a **shared** chip with the owner's initials. Drag it into one of your own folders like any
other chat; where you file it is yours alone. A chat of yours that has other people in it shows a
plain **shared** chip. When you leave a chat, or its owner removes you, it leaves your list.

### Share by asking the agent

You can ask for shares in one message, for example: *share Acme with @Joe and share this chat with
him*.

- Enter asks you once whether to share the chat with Joe. That is the only question.
- The agent grants the workspace with its `workspace_member_add` tool and replies in one line, for
  example *Joe can now read and write Acme; he's in this chat from now on.*
- The agent grants a workspace only when your message asks for it, and only if you own that
  workspace.
- It names people only by the account your **@** mention carried. A name it was not given an account
  for is not looked up.

When you ask the agent to share a chat only you are in, for example *share this chat with
@Eugenia*, it first answers in one line, naming anything sensitive it sees in the history, for
example *This chat has usage and payment figures and an internal memo. Share it all, share from a
point, or start a new chat with her?* Your answer decides:

- **all** (the default for a plain "share this chat"): she joins this chat and sees its history.
- **from a point**: a new shared chat starts with what was said from that point on.
- **fresh**: a new shared chat starts empty.

The agent also has `chat_participants`, `chat_participant_add` and `chat_participant_remove`. Every
add and remove needs you present (a scheduled run cannot do it) and follows the same rules as the
message box. Sharing a chat never gives anyone a workspace.

### Share a routine's chat

A routine's chat (the thread its scheduled runs post into) is shared the same way: mention someone in
it and choose **Share and send**.

- The routine stays yours. Only you can pause, edit or delete it.
- Everyone in the chat sees every run and can reply. A reply steers the next run.
- Once others are in the chat, each run uses only what everyone there can read. Your Gmail, Calendar,
  meetings and other connections are refused (`shared_conversation`).
- If the routine reads your private connections, the question says so first, for example *This
  routine reads your Gmail. Shared, it can't — its runs would only use what everyone here can see.* The
  button then reads **Share anyway**.
- A run that needs a refused read ends refused and the routine pauses, with one line in the chat
  naming who it is shared with and what could not be read. Unshare the chat, or move the routine to
  a private chat, then resume it ([Routines](/flows/routines#share-a-routines-chat)).
- A routine that reads only workspaces everyone in the chat can read, or public sources, keeps
  working as before.
- Its earlier runs stay in your private chat history. The shared chat starts with the message you
  sent.

### Talk with the agent and with each other

- In a chat with only you, the agent answers every message.
- With two or more people, the agent answers when you write **@vexa** (or an agent's name) or address
  it directly (*vexa, …*). Other messages stay in the chat for everyone to read, and the agent reads
  them before its next answer.
- It is one conversation: what people write to each other and the agent's turns are shown
  together in time order, each with who wrote it. The box says who a message reaches, for example
  *Message Alex and Vexa*. The same notice repeated by a routine shows once, with a count and
  its times. Suggestions appear only in a new, empty chat.
- An admin can make the agent answer every message with the `session_agent_speaks` policy
  (`always` or `mentioned`) in `_global/POLICIES.md`. It applies to the whole deployment; there is no
  per-workspace setting.

### See each other live

- A message you post without addressing the agent shows to everyone in the chat within a few
  seconds, with the name of who wrote it, and is still there after a reload.
- When another person's turn with the agent finishes, your chat reads the conversation again and
  shows their question and the answer.
- The agent in a shared chat may read the transcript of a meeting that was sent into a workspace
  every person in the chat reads, and may send a bot into a meeting bound to such a workspace. A
  meeting kept private stays out.

### What the agent may use in a shared chat

Everyone in the chat reads every answer, so in a chat with other people the agent uses only the
workspaces **every** participant can read. Your Personal workspace and your private `_system` workspace are never
used there. If your question needs something only you can read, the agent says it can't use it here
and offers to answer in your private chat.

To use one specific page anyway, share it for that message (the `share` field of
[`POST /agent/chat`](/api/agent), `workspace/path`, up to five). The page is read with your access and
quoted into that turn only.

### Your private sources stay out of shared workspaces

In your own chat the agent may read your mail, calendar and connected services. If it then writes a
page into a workspace someone else also reads, nothing is written: the agent tells you what it is
holding back, from which workspace and who else reads it, with a short code. Reply `confirm <code>`
to write that one page; anything else stays off the shared workspace. Pages on your own desk are
written as usual.

## Limits and errors

| You see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *Sharing chats is not available on this deployment.* | agent-api has no database configured | an operator sets `DB_HOST` (below) |
| *Only the chat's owner can add people.* (in the share question) | you are a member of someone else's chat | send without sharing, or ask the owner |
| Nobody is listed after **@** | you share no workspace with anyone yet | share a workspace first; inviting someone new is done from the workspace |
| `403 session_read_only` | you were added as a viewer | ask the owner to make you a member |
| `404 session not found` | you are not in that chat | ask to be added |

A chat holds one conversation: if two people's turns finish on the same version at once, the second
save is refused and logged rather than merged, and that turn's reply stays in its own stream.

## How it works

### Where a conversation lives

| Data | Single writer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Session (owner, title, participants, roles, flow, channel) | agent-api | the instance's Postgres, `agent_session*` tables, reached as the store's own role |
| Transcript versions (the harness's files and your turn records) | agent-api | the store's own bucket (`vexa-sessions`), under `sessions/`, one object per turn version |
| Messages the agent was not asked to answer | agent-api | its database |
| The conversation during a turn | the agent worker | a private folder under the worker's home, outside every workspace |

### A chat becoming shared

Turns made while you were alone used everything you can read, so what the next person sees of that
conversation is the owner's decision. `POST /agent/sessions/{id}/participants` on a chat only its
owner is in takes `history`:

| `history` | What happens |
|---|---|
| `all` | The person joins this session. They read its history, and their turns continue its transcript. |
| `from:<turn>` | A new session carries the visible text from that turn (its index in the history) on, as messages. |
| `none` | A new session starts at version 0, with no transcript. |

Without `history` the owner's add is `all`. `share-preview` returns the options and the private reads
the history holds. The person added gets a "chat shared with you" notification and the chat in their
rail, under **Shared with me**.
Carried messages are only the words a person typed and the agent's visible replies, never tool calls
or their results. `excerpt` (explicit turn indices) still works, in place of `history`.

Anyone but the owner adding a person starts a new empty session that they own. Naming `history` is
refused with `403` `history_is_the_owners`. A viewer adds nobody.

From the moment a second person is in, every turn follows the shared-turn rule below. No workspace
is granted: the newcomer's turns run without the workspaces they cannot read. An address with no
account gets the same answer as one with an account, and nobody is added.

### Mentions and the share question

1. The first **@** fetches `GET /agent/people/mentionable`: every member of every group workspace you
   are a member of, from each workspace's `policy/members.json`, with their account id. `_global` and
   `_system` do not count, so being in the same organisation lists nobody.
2. A message sends its mentions as `mentions` (account ids) on [`POST /agent/chat`](/api/agent).
   agent-api keeps only ids from your list and tells the agent who each one is.
3. On Enter, the message box reads the chat's participants. If a mentioned person is missing and you
   are the owner, it reads `GET /agent/sessions/{id}/share-preview`: whether the chat forks, whether it
   is a routine's chat, and which private reads its turns have made or are asked to make.
4. **Share and send** calls `POST /agent/sessions/{id}/participants` with `subject` for each person,
   and `history: all` on the first add to a private chat. **New chat with them** sends `history:
   none`: the first add answers a new `session_id`, the message box opens that chat, and the message
   is sent there with `to_agent`. A person by id must be on your list; anyone else gets the answer an
   unknown address gets, and nobody is added.

### A routine's chat becoming shared

The new shared session takes the routine's flow binding and the thread name its runs are posted to,
so every later run lands in the shared chat. The private session keeps the earlier runs under a new
name and is no longer bound. A run is posted by flows over the internal tier, so agent-api answers
it without a mention; it is still a shared turn. A routine made with `params.thread: "new"` starts a
fresh thread for each run, and those threads are not shared.

### Workspace grants by the agent

`workspace_member_add` (`POST /agent/workspace/members/add`) takes a workspace slug or name, a person
(the account id a mention carried, or the email of an existing account) and `read` or `write`
(stored as `reader` or `contributor`). Only the workspace's owner adds. `_system` and `_global` are
refused. A person with no account is refused and pointed to `workspace_invite`; this tool invites
nobody.

### A turn

1. agent-api checks the session: you must be a participant with a member or owner role.
2. With other people in the chat, agent-api decides whether the agent answers (`session_agent_speaks`).
   If not, the message is stored for everyone and nothing runs.
3. agent-api works out the workspaces every participant can read. The worker is started with only
   those mounted, and its tools' ceiling is the same set. A change of participants starts a new worker.
   The turn is marked *shared*, so the worker is also refused everything that is yours alone: your
   connections (Gmail, Calendar and any other), their tokens and secrets, and your meetings,
   transcripts, recordings and bots, and your flows, reactions and timeline. The agent says it cannot use them here and offers your private
   chat. A workspace page you share for the turn is read with your own access and put into the prompt.
4. The turn's input carries the conversation (`SessionBundle` in unit.v1). The worker writes it into
   its private folder and runs claude-code, codex or openai-agent with that folder as the place they
   keep their history.
5. Before the turn completes, the worker hands the conversation back (`session-bundle`). agent-api saves
   it as the next version only if the version it was based on is still the latest. The browser never
   receives it.

**One turn at a time.** A chat runs one turn at a time. A message that would start a second turn while
one is running is refused with `409 session_conflict`; send it again when the answer arrives. If a
turn's save is still refused, the chat's history (`GET /api/sessions/{id}/history`) shows a
`session_conflict` fault until the next turn saves, and the chat shows it as the last row, so a
lost reply is never silent. In a chat only you write, a reply whose save found a newer version is saved
onto it whenever it carries the stored conversation further, so you are not asked to send it again.

### One stream

The chat reads `GET /api/sessions/{id}/messages` every few seconds (lines people wrote and their
names) and `GET /api/sessions/{id}/history` (the agent's turns, each with `at`, when it was said,
taken from the conversation's own record). The browser places each line before the first turn said
after it; a turn without a time takes its neighbour's. agent-api stays the one writer of both; the
browser only orders them. A person who is not in the chat gets `404` from both
(`core/agent/tests/test_access_sessions_share.py` · `test_the_newcomer_cannot_read_the_private_session`).
Tests: `core/agent/tests/test_history_turn_times.py`;
`clients/terminal/src/surfaces/__tests__/sharedConversation.test.tsx`, `repeatedNotices.test.tsx`;
`clients/terminal/src/minutes/__tests__/startersOnlyNew.test.ts`.

### Private sources and shared writes

A session that calls a private-source route (mail, calendar, a connected service, onboarding
research) is marked for 12 hours, in agent-api for its tool writes (`workspace_write`,
`entity_upsert`) and in the worker for the files the turn writes itself. A write from a marked
session into a workspace whose `policy/members.json` names anyone besides the person is refused
(`409 private_sources_to_shared`, with `confirm_code`); at the commit the worker puts that workspace
back and keeps the change on the person's desk under `.vexa/held-writes/` (git-excluded). The
person's own chat message `confirm <code>` releases that write once: agent-api takes the code only
from a chat message the person sent themselves, never from a delegated caller, and the worker writes
held files back when the turn it runs carries the code. Marks and codes live in agent-api's memory
(one replica), so a restart forgets them.

### Live lines, meetings and bots in a shared chat

- **Lines.** A message that does not address the agent is stored by agent-api as a note on the
  conversation, holding only the person's words (the terminal's context line is cut off). The chat
  reads `GET /api/sessions/{session}/messages` every 3 seconds while the tab is visible (every 15
  seconds while the store does not hold the chat yet) and at once after it posts a line. The answer
  carries `you`, the conversation's `version` and `names` (each person's roster name). agent-api is
  the only writer of notes and of the version; the terminal only reads them.
- **Another person's turn.** When `version` moves and this tab is not in a turn of its own, the
  chat reads the conversation history again and replaces what it shows.
- **A meeting's transcript and a bot.** meeting-api refuses a shared turn every person-wide route.
  Three requests pass that app-wide rule and are decided again by their route: `GET
  /transcripts/by-id/{id}` and `GET /transcripts/{platform}/{native_meeting_id}` answer only for a
  meeting bound to a workspace inside the turn's ceiling (the workspaces every participant reads),
  and `POST /bots` only for a bot bound to such a workspace. Transcript search and meeting lists stay
  refused. The ceiling arrives on the gateway-signed delegation headers.
- **A member reads a meeting by its link.** When the caller owns no meeting on that link,
  `GET /transcripts/{platform}/{native_meeting_id}` takes the newest one bound to a workspace the
  caller is a member of and reads it through the same by-id access decision.

### Configuration

agent-api reaches the store with credentials of its own, never the instance's:

- **Database.** A login role, `vexa_agent_sessions` by default, with no admin power and grants on the
  `agent_session*` tables only. The migrations path creates it with the admin credential:
  `python -m control_plane.session_schema init` (the compose `agent-sessions-init` service, the Helm
  `agent-sessions-schema` Job, the Lite entrypoint). agent-api runs no DDL; it refuses a superuser
  login and a schema version it does not know, and the store stays off.
- **Objects.** Its own bucket, `vexa-sessions`, with a key scoped to that bucket alone. On the bundled
  storage, storage-init creates both and proves the key is refused everywhere in the recordings bucket.
  Transcripts go to S3 only; there is no directory store.

| | Compose | Helm | Lite |
|---|---|---|---|
| On by default | yes | no: the chart makes no bucket | yes, with object storage |
| `VEXA_SESSIONS_DB_PASSWORD` | minted into `.env` by `make up` | generated Secret, or `agentApi.sessions.existingSecret` | minted into the state dir |
| `VEXA_SESSIONS_S3_ACCESS_KEY`, `_SECRET_KEY` | minted into `.env`; scoped by storage-init | `agentApi.sessions.s3.existingSecret` (you make the bucket and key) | minted into the state dir; scoped by the entrypoint |
| `VEXA_SESSIONS_S3_BUCKET` | `vexa-sessions` | `agentApi.sessions.s3.bucket` | `vexa-sessions` |
| `VEXA_SESSIONS_S3_ENDPOINT` | `S3_ENDPOINT` if set, else the `storage` service | `storage.s3.endpoint` | `MINIO_ENDPOINT` |

With your own S3 on compose, create the bucket and a key limited to it and set the three
`VEXA_SESSIONS_S3_*` values. Rendering and storage-init refuse the recordings bucket or a storage root
key as the store's. `VEXA_SESSIONS_DB_URL` (`postgresql://…`) overrides the database parts. Without
the role's password or the store's key, the store is off and chats keep the old layout. agent-api
holds one database connection per replica.

### Upgrading

Older builds kept chats inside workspaces (`.claude/` in your private `_system` workspace or your
desk). Existing chats keep working: for one release agent-api reads that old layout when the store
holds nothing for a chat, and the first turn afterwards saves it into the store. To move every chat at
once, run the migration where agent-api runs:

```bash
# Docker Compose
docker compose exec agent-api python -m control_plane.session_migrate --dry-run

# Helm
kubectl exec deploy/<release>-vexa-agent-api -- python -m control_plane.session_migrate --dry-run

# Vexa Lite: agent-api runs under supervisord; the command takes the store settings the entrypoint
# set (read from PID 1's environment as data, so a value holding a quote or a space is passed intact)
docker exec -w /app/agent vexa-lite /opt/venvs/agent/bin/python -c 'import os
e = dict(x.split("=", 1) for x in open("/proc/1/environ").read().split("\0") if "=" in x)
env = {k: v for k, v in e.items() if k in ("DB_HOST", "DB_PORT", "DB_NAME") or k.startswith("VEXA_SESSIONS_")}
env.update(PATH=os.environ["PATH"], PYTHONPATH="/app/agent", VEXA_WORKSPACES_DIR="/workspaces")
os.execve("/opt/venvs/agent/bin/python", ["python", "-m", "control_plane.session_migrate", "--dry-run"], env)'
```

| Flag | Effect |
|---|---|
| `--dry-run` | counts only |
| *(none)* | copy and verify by reading back |
| `--clean` | also remove the old files, only where verified |
| `--clean --retire` | move every old-layout chat file out of the workspaces, after the store's copy is verified; a copy that diverged from the store's is moved too. Nothing is deleted: each file goes to `<workspaces>/.sessions-retired/<run>/`, a folder no workspace, mount or person ever names |
| `--restore` | move every file `--retire` moved back to where it was; a file written at that name since is never overwritten (it stays archived and is counted) |
| `--workspaces <dir>` | the workspaces volume, if not agent-api's own |

Without a configured store the command exits with code 2 and migrates nothing.

It is safe to run more than once; a chat already in the store is skipped. It logs counts, never names
or content, and ends with `left_in_workspaces`: how many old-layout chat files are still inside a
workspace (0 after `--clean --retire`).

**Recommended upgrade:** back up the workspaces volume, run `--dry-run`, then `--clean --retire`, and
check that `copied` + `skipped` equals `threads` and `left_in_workspaces` is 0. Once a release has
passed with no `--restore` needed, the archive can be removed by the operator. A forget
(*"forget this"*) redacts the archive as well as the store.

`--restore` is for rolling back to a build that reads chats from workspaces. It puts the files back
as they were when retired; turns taken since live only in the store. While the store is on, restored
files are chat files under a mount again, so run `--clean --retire` again once the rollback is over.
The archive holds the only copy of a diverged file (the run counts them as `diverged`); keep it until
you have checked those.

## Why it complies

### Architecture

| Rule | How this design meets it | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P2** contracts | the conversation's wire shapes are `SessionBundle` and `SessionBundleEvent` in unit.v1, with goldens | `gate:schema`, `gate:contract-version` |
| **P18** fail loud | a second concurrent turn is a typed 409; a refused save is a `session_conflict` fault in the history; a store that cannot answer refuses a shared chat or a store id with 503, and only a private chat falls back to the old layout | deny tests below |
| **P20** authorize every access | the session gate (participants and roles) and the workspace gate (your own membership) are separate; sharing a chat grants no workspace | deny tests below |
| **P23** one writer per carrier | agent-api is the only writer of the store; the worker only hands the conversation back on its own output stream | `gate:dataflow` |

### Security

**Who can do what.** Participants read a chat; members and the owner post; only the owner shares
a chat's history or removes people; a member who adds someone starts a new chat of their own; anyone
may leave. A participant's turn runs as that participant, on their own
workspaces, narrowed to what everyone in the chat can read.

**Protections.**

- A shared chat's turn mounts neither the speaker's Personal workspace nor `_system`, and its tools cannot reach a
  workspace outside the shared set.
- A shared chat's turn is refused the speaker's connections, secrets, meetings and flows, by agent-api, the
  credential broker, meeting-api and flows-api (`shared_conversation`), not only by the prompt.
- A worker can save only into the chat and version it was handed, and only while the speaker can
  still write.
- A conversation never reaches a workspace volume or a browser; workers never hold object-store
  credentials. With the store on, a worker keeps a turn's harness state in a private folder outside
  every mount, and the agent reads another chat only through `chats_search` and `chat_read`, which
  apply these rules; there is no chat file under any mount for it to find.
- agent-api holds neither the database's admin credential nor the recordings bucket's key: its role
  reads and writes the `agent_session*` tables only, and its key reaches its own bucket only.
- Bundle paths are plain relative paths; anything else is refused, never repaired.
- A private chat's history is shared only by its owner (an agent in the owner's own turn names what
  in it is sensitive first). Shared from a point or
  fresh, the new chat carries no tool call, tool result or composed prompt, and nobody but the owner
  can read the private one.
- Adding and removing people needs a person in the loop: a scheduled or background worker is
  refused (`human_session_required`). An agent acting in your own typed turn may add, as you, under
  the same rules.
- The @ list and every add by id are limited to the people you share a group workspace with. Nobody
  else is named, and an id outside that set adds nobody.
- A workspace is granted only by its owner, only to an existing account, and only when the message
  asks for it.
- A routine's runs in a shared chat are shared turns: the routine cannot read its owner's private
  connections there, and only its owner can change or stop it.

### Lines, meeting reads and bots from a shared chat

| Rule | How this design meets it | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P20** authorize every access | a chat's lines answer only to its participants (the session gate); a shared turn reaches one meeting's transcript, or sends a bot, only for a workspace inside its signed ceiling, checked in the route after the app-wide rule lets the request through | deny tests below |
| **P23** one writer per carrier | agent-api writes notes and the conversation's version; meeting-api writes meetings; the terminal only reads | `gate:dataflow` |
| **P18** fail loud | a refused read or bot is the same `shared_conversation` refusal as every other person-wide route | ungated: reviewed |
