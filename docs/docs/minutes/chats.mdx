The middle column of Minutes is a chat with your agent. The agent reads the workspaces mounted in the
chat and writes into one of them. This page explains how a turn behaves, what happens to messages you
send while it works, how long acts run as background jobs, and how to choose where a chat writes.

## What you can do

### Send a message

1. Type in the composer (*Type / for skills, or ask the agent…*). In a meeting chat the placeholder
   follows the meeting: preparing, live, or after.
2. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to send; <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> starts a new line. The composer
   also has **Attach files**, **Dictate** and **Send**.
3. While the agent works, one line under its reply shows what it is doing and how many steps it has
   taken. The composer itself shows no status; it only offers **Stop**.
4. When the turn finishes, the reply is rendered in full (notes, cards, steps, tabs and wiki links).
   Every file the turn wrote appears as a chip you can open, and a commit shows as
   *committed · `<sha>`*.

**Stop** stops your browser from reading the turn. It does not cancel work already handed to the agent.

The transcript follows new output while you are at the bottom. Scroll up and it stays where you are;
**Jump to latest** brings you back.

### Send while the agent is busy

You never have to wait. A message you send during a turn goes straight to the agent's inbox and shows
at the foot of the chat as *queued · … · queued behind the current turn*. Queued messages run in the
order you sent them. A reload does not lose them: the list is read back from the server.

If the agent cannot take a message, the row turns red: *blocked — `<source>` · `<kind>` — `<detail>`*,
with **Retry**. If the network dropped the message before the server saw it, your browser keeps a copy
and re-sends it when the chat is idle or the page reloads.

### Background jobs

**Extend** and **Create** on a [page](/minutes/pages#extend-or-create-a-page-with-the-agent) run as
*jobs*: the agent works on the page in the background, and the chat stays free.

- A running job shows at the foot of the chat as *job · `<workspace/path>` · N steps · `<label>`*, and
  also on the card you pressed.
- When it finishes, the agent posts one line in the chat and the page refreshes. If it fails, a line
  says so.
- Pressing a second act on the same page queues it behind the first (*queued behind the one running*).
  Pressing the same act again joins the running job instead of starting another.
- There is no cancel. A job stops early only if the agent restarts; the chat then says the job stopped
  and that you can ask again.
- The start and finish lines are live only. After a reload, look at the page itself.

If the connection drops mid-job, the chat says *Lost the connection to that background job — it may
still have finished; reopen the page to see.*

### Suggestions in an empty chat

An empty chat shows up to **four** suggestions. Each one names a real thing and does it in one click.
They are ranked by how useful they are right now:

| Suggestion | When it shows | A click |
|---|---|---|
| *Catch up: `<meeting>`, live now* | a meeting is live | asks for a catch-up in this chat, bound to that meeting |
| *Prep: `<meeting>`, today 3:00 PM* | a meeting starts later today, or within two hours | asks for a brief |
| *Recap: `<meeting>`, yesterday 3:00 PM* | the newest meeting from the last 7 days that nobody has asked about | asks for decisions, owners and open items |
| *2 meetings to review: `<name>`, `<name>`* | the rail is hiding new rows (the same count as its **All** filter) | shows the hidden rows |
| items your agents proposed | an agent filed a job for you | runs the job; **×** (*Not now*) dismisses it |
| *My email is …, set up a workspace for me* | your desk has nothing in it | starts setup |
| *Connect Google Calendar* · *Reconnect Gmail* · *Finish connecting …* | a Google connection is missing, disconnected, or waiting for your consent | opens **Connections** on that account |
| *Send Vexa to a meeting* | there is room in the row | opens a field: paste a Google Meet, Zoom, Teams or Jitsi link and press **Send bot** |

A meeting is named by its title. A meeting with no title is named by its platform, for example
*Google Meet call*, and always with when it happened. The raw meeting code is never shown.

When nothing is waiting, the chat shows one line, *Nothing is waiting for you. Ask me anything, or send
Vexa to a meeting.*, and the send action.

Pressing a suggestion acts in the same chat. A meeting suggestion binds the chat to that meeting, or
opens that meeting's own chat if this one already belongs to another meeting. A used suggestion is not
offered again in that chat. Opening **Connections** or the paste field does not use the suggestion up.

### Links in replies

Every page a reply names opens on click: wiki links, `.md` paths and workspace paths. A link to a page
that does not exist yet opens its path, where you can [create it](/minutes/pages#extend-or-create-a-page-with-the-agent).

### Choose where a chat writes

The bar above the chat reads *Chat ›* or *Meeting ›* followed by the chat's name, then one chip per
workspace in the chat.

- The highlighted chip is the **target**: *Writes in this chat go here. The others are mounted to
  read.* Click another chip to make it the target.
- **+** (*Add a workspace to this chat*) mounts another workspace; a chip's remove button takes it out.
- With no target set, writes go to your own desk.
- Org admins can also choose **Company** (the `_global` workspace).
- You can also say "work in `<workspace>`" and the agent moves the target itself.

The target is where writes go by default. It never grants a right: a workspace you cannot write is
refused as a target.

### Name, order and delete chats

A chat is named from your first message until you rename it. Renaming, the activity dot, reordering,
reopening history and deleting a chat are covered in [Manage your chats](/how-to/chat-controls). What
happens when a long turn reaches its tool-call limit is in [Long agent turns](/agent-tool-calls).

## Limits and errors

| You see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *That isn't a Google Meet, Zoom, Teams or Jitsi link…* | the pasted text is not a meeting link the bot can join | check the link; your text stays in the field |
| *That message did not reach your agent — nothing was lost on your side, please send it again.* | the server could not hand the message to the agent | send it again |
| *No model credentials are configured, so the agent cannot run…* | the deployment has no model provider | an operator adds one ([model credentials](/model-credentials-licensing)) |
| a red block *`<Source>` · `<kind>`* with **Retry** | a dependency failed; the turn ended with a typed fault | read the remedy line; retry |
| *the terminal could not reach the Vexa gateway* | the gateway is down or unreachable | try again in a moment |
| *not a workspace you can write* | the chosen target is read-only for you | pick a workspace where you are a contributor |
| *only an org admin may write into _global* | **Company** is admin-only | ask an admin |
| *Lost the connection to that background job…* | the stream dropped during a job | reopen the page |

Other limits: a chat id is 1–128 letters, digits, `.`, `_` or `-`; the queue lists at most 200 waiting
messages and none older than an hour; a chat shows at most 4 suggestions. A chat turn is started by a
person: an agent acting for someone cannot start one.

## How it works

### Flow

1. **Send.** The browser posts to the terminal's `/api/chat`. The terminal forwards to the gateway's
   `/agent/chat` with your API key from the sign-in cookie. The gateway resolves the key to you and
   forwards to agent-api `/api/chat` with your identity signed.
2. **Prepare.** agent-api takes the user from the signed identity, never from the body. It keeps your
   words as typed, adds any context (the target workspace, a job or flow mark) after a separator, checks
   that model credentials exist, and records the chat in your session list.
3. **Dispatch.** agent-api writes the message into the chat's inbox and starts or wakes the agent
   worker. It then relays the worker's output stream back to the browser as server-sent events. A
   reconnect resumes from the last event it saw; nothing is dispatched twice.
4. **Queue.** A message sent mid-turn goes to `/api/chat/submit`, which runs the same steps without
   streaming. `/api/chat/pending` lists what is still waiting, and whether the queue is blocked and why.
5. **Jobs.** For `create`, `extend` and `extend_transcript`, the worker runs the act on its own thread,
   reports `job-started`, progress and `job-done` or `job-failed` on the chat's stream, and ends the
   chat turn at once so the chat can answer.

### Empty-chat suggestions

1. **Read.** On load the terminal reads four things it already has a route for: the meetings list, the
   chat list (`GET /api/sessions`), your desk's facts (`GET /api/workspace/desk`) and short list
   (`GET /api/proposals`), and your connections (`GET /api/connections/list`, metadata only: provider,
   label, status). It re-reads connections when the tab regains focus or a consent window reports back.
2. **Decide.** One pure function, `proposals()` in `clients/terminal/src/minutes/proposals.ts`, ranks the
   suggestions and cuts the row to four. It makes no request and asks no model.
3. **Name.** A meeting is named by its title; with none, by its platform, plus a relative time
   (`relativeWhen`). The fallback title the meetings list builds from the meeting code is never shown.
4. **Connections.** `connectionGap()` reads the broker's statuses: `ready` shows nothing, `disconnected`
   offers **Reconnect**, `awaiting_user` offers **Finish connecting**, and no Google Calendar at all offers
   **Connect Google Calendar**. If the list has not loaded, nothing is offered.
5. **Act.** A click goes through `applyProposal()`: a meeting or job suggestion sends a turn in this chat,
   review flips the rail filter, a connect suggestion opens the Connections panel, and the send action
   opens a paste field. A pasted link is checked by the same parser the meetings list uses
   (`parseMeetingInput`) before it becomes your message; the agent then sends the bot with
   `request_meeting_bot`.

### Who writes what

| Data | Single writer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Your chat list (rail) and names | agent-api | Redis, per user |
| Rail order | agent-api | Redis, per user |
| Chat inbox | agent-api (every entry signed) | the chat unit's input stream |
| Turn output and job events | the agent worker | the chat unit's output stream; agent-api only relays it |
| Inbox position | the agent worker | the chat unit's cursor |
| Job records | the agent worker | the chat's working folder |
| Suggestions filed by agents | agent-api | `.vexa/proposals.json` on your desk |
| Connection status | credential broker | the broker's database; the terminal only reads it |
| The ranked suggestion row | nobody: it is derived in the browser on each render and never stored | — |
| Open tabs, view, unsent copies | your browser | `localStorage` |

### Routes

| Browser calls (terminal) | agent-api (through the gateway's `/agent/*`) |
|---|---|
| `POST /api/chat` (streamed) | `POST /api/chat` |
| `POST /api/chat/submit` | `POST /api/chat/submit` |
| `GET /api/chat/pending` | `GET /api/chat/pending` |
| `POST /api/chat/target` | `POST /api/chat/target` |
| `POST /api/chat/name` | `POST /api/chat/name` |
| `GET`, `PUT /api/chat/order` | `GET`, `PUT /api/chat/order` |
| `GET /api/sessions`, `/api/sessions/{id}/history` | the same paths |
| `GET`, `POST /api/proposals`, `POST /api/proposals/resolve` | the same paths |
| `GET /api/connections/list` | the credential broker's `GET /api/connections` (terminal server only) |

`/agent/chat` is a streamed route of its own in `core/agent/routes.v1.json`; the others pass through the
gateway's `/agent/*` forward. The request and event shapes are in [the agent API](/api/agent).

### Configuration

`GATEWAY_URL` on the terminal server. The job and tool-budget limits are worker settings
(`VEXA_AGENT_JOB_MAX_TOOL_CALLS` and related, see `core/agent/llm/JOBS.md`).

## Why it complies

### Architecture

| Rule | How this design meets it | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P2** contracts · **P6** one front door | the browser calls only the terminal's `/api/*`; the terminal calls only the gateway | `gate:isolation`, `gate:exports`, `gate:domain-doors` |
| **P18** fail loud | a refused spawn, a provider refusal or a gateway outage ends as a typed fault (`source` + `kind`) shown in the chat; the terminal never passes a bare *Internal Server Error*; a blocked queue says what blocked it; a failed job posts a line | `gate:fault-surfacing` (under `gate:node` and `gate:python`) |
| **P20** authorize every access | the user is the signed identity; the target must be a workspace you can write; suggestions are your own desk's | deny tests below |
| **P21** state from evidence | the activity line and job rows come from the worker's events, not from the button press. The connect suggestion reads the broker's own status, never a deployment flag, and offers nothing while that status is unknown | `proposals.test.ts` ("hidden when Google Calendar and Gmail are connected", "hidden while the list has not answered"); otherwise ungated: reviewed |
| **P23** one writer per carrier | agent-api is the inbox's only writer; the worker is the output stream's only writer and agent-api relays it unchanged. The suggestion row is derived, never stored, and re-derives nobody's data: connection status stays the broker's | `gate:dataflow` |
| **P18** fail loud (suggestions) | an unreadable connections list shows no connect suggestion rather than a wrong one; a bad pasted link is refused beside the field with your text kept | `proposalChips.test.tsx` ("anything else is refused beside the field, with the input kept") |

### Security

**Who can do what.** Only you can post into your chats. The chat's target is a default, never a grant:
writes are still checked against your rights in that workspace, and an agent acting for you is held to
the workspaces of the chat that dispatched it. Only an org admin can target the company workspace.

**Protections.**

- The user comes from the gateway-signed identity; an unsigned or missing identity is refused, and a
  `subject` in the body is ignored.
- Unknown request fields are refused, so no field can widen a request.
- A message composed by Vexa or dispatched by a flow is marked, and never shown as your words.
- An agent acting for someone cannot start a chat turn.
- Job events carry their chat's id, so one chat's job never shows in another.
- Suggestions read only your own meetings, desk and connections. The connections list returns
  metadata (provider, label, status), never a token or any mail or calendar content, and only the
  signed-in person's rows. Connecting still happens in the Connections panel, on your click.
- A pasted meeting link is sent only after it parses as a Google Meet, Zoom, Teams or Jitsi link.
