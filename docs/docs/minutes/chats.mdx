The middle column of Minutes is a chat with your agent. The agent reads the workspaces mounted in the
chat and writes into one of them. This page explains how a turn behaves, what happens to messages you
send while it works, how long acts run as background jobs, and how to choose where a chat writes.

## What you can do

### Send a message

1. Type in the composer (*Type / for skills, or ask the agent…*). In a meeting chat the placeholder
   follows the meeting: preparing, live, or after.
2. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to send; <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> starts a new line. The composer
   also has **Attach files**, **Dictate** and **Send**.
3. While the agent works, one line under its reply shows what it is doing and how many steps it has
   taken. The composer itself shows no status; it only offers **Stop**.
4. When the turn finishes, the reply is rendered in full (notes, cards, steps, tabs and wiki links).
   Every file the turn wrote appears as a chip you can open, and a commit shows as
   *committed · `<sha>`*.

**Stop** stops your browser from reading the turn. It does not cancel work already handed to the agent.

The transcript follows new output while you are at the bottom. Scroll up and it stays where you are;
**Jump to latest** brings you back.

### Send while the agent is busy

You never have to wait. A message you send during a turn goes straight to the agent's inbox and shows
at the foot of the chat as *queued · … · queued behind the current turn*. Queued messages run in the
order you sent them. A reload does not lose them: the list is read back from the server.

A queued row shows your own words. **Send now** on a row stops the current answer (the chat says it
stopped) and runs that message next.

A meeting link, or a bot command such as *send bot* or *stop the bot*, never waits: it is carried out
at once, beside the running answer, and gets its own one-line reply (*Bot sent to Zoom 92446951537 —
waiting for admission*). The running answer and your next message are told what happened.

Every queued message is answered, and the answer appears in its own reply under your message, even
when the agent starts on it while it is still finishing the previous reply. If your chat was already
idle when the agent started it, the chat attaches to it and shows it the same way.

A model change, or a new workspace the agent created, takes effect from the next message you send
when the chat is idle. A message you send while a turn runs queues on the agent that is running.

If the agent cannot take a message, the row turns red: *blocked — `<source>` · `<kind>` — `<detail>`*,
with **Retry**. If the network dropped the message before the server saw it, your browser keeps a copy
and re-sends it when the chat is idle or the page reloads.

### Simple asks run at once

In your own chat, a short message that is one simple ask is carried out without starting the agent:

- *call @megan*, *call Megan*, *start a call with Megan*: the call starts and rings her, exactly as if
  the agent had called `call_person`. The chat says *Calling Megan…* with the room.
- *open the OeNB page*: the page opens when exactly one page in your workspaces is called that.

Anything else goes to the agent as before: a question, a message with more than one request, a name
that matches several people or nobody, a page that is not found. *Invite Ana* and *schedule a call with
Ana* always go to the agent, which asks before anything is sent. A message that sends, posts, shares or
deletes something is never carried out this way. Rooms, shared chats, routines and messages sent
without watching the chat are never routed.

### Dictate a message

**Dictate** (or <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>) listens and writes
what you say into the box as you speak. Nothing is sent until you press Enter. If you press Enter
while it is still listening, the message is sent and the box empties; listening continues, and only
what you say after the send appears. Text you type while it listens is kept, and dictation continues
after it. Stop with the button or <kbd>Esc</kbd>.

**How it works.** The browser sends the microphone's audio in short windows to `POST /api/stt` and
shows the recognised text plus a live draft. When the shown text leaves the box (sent, or taken over
by your edit), the composer tells the dictation engine to commit: answers that arrive later for audio
before that moment are dropped, so sent words never come back. The committed text is kept only as
context for recognising what follows. One module writes the box's dictated text,
`clients/terminal/src/ui-kit/composerDictation.ts`.

**Why it complies.** P21, state from evidence: the box shows only text recognised after the last
send (`clients/terminal/src/ui-kit/__tests__/dictationSend.test.ts`: "empties the composer and keeps
it empty when the pre-send final answers late", "keeps a person's edit; later speech is appended
after it, not over it"). Ungated, reviewed. No new route, contract or stored data.

### Background jobs

**Extend** and **Create** on a [page](/minutes/pages#extend-or-create-a-page-with-the-agent) run as
*jobs*: the agent works on the page in the background, and the chat stays free.

- A running job shows at the foot of the chat as *job · `<workspace/path>` · N steps · `<label>`*, and
  also on the card you pressed.
- When it finishes, the agent posts one line in the chat and the page refreshes. If it fails, a line
  says so.
- Pressing a second act on the same page queues it behind the first (*queued behind the one running*).
  Pressing the same act again joins the running job instead of starting another.
- There is no cancel. A job stops early only if the agent restarts; the chat then says the job stopped
  and that you can ask again.
- The start and finish lines are live only. After a reload, look at the page itself.

If the connection drops mid-job, the chat says *Lost the connection to that background job — it may
still have finished; reopen the page to see.*

### Suggestions in an empty chat

An empty chat suggests what to do with your email and calendar, and offers a voice note. What it
offers depends on what you have connected. Each suggestion starts with an icon for what it does: a
calendar for meeting prep, mail for the email check, a plug for connecting an account, a microphone
for a voice note.

| Suggestion | When it shows | A click |
|---|---|---|
| *Connect your email* | no Gmail is connected (or one is waiting for your consent, or was disconnected) | opens **Connections** on Gmail |
| *Connect your calendar* | no Google Calendar is connected (same) | opens **Connections** on Google Calendar |
| *Check my email* | Gmail is connected | the agent reads your recent inbox and tells you which messages need a reply from you |
| *Prepare for `<title>` at 3:00 PM* | Google Calendar is connected and a meeting with a title starts in the next 24 hours | the agent reads the event, then your transcripts and workspaces for what is open with those people, and briefs you |
| *Record a voice note* | always, after the suggestions above | opens the recorder; see [Voice notes](/minutes/voice-notes) |
| items your agents proposed | an agent filed a job for you | runs the job; **×** (*Not now*) dismisses it |
| *My email is …, set up a workspace for me* | your Personal workspace has nothing in it | starts setup |

The meeting is always named by its calendar title, and the next titled meeting is the one offered.
A meeting with no title is never offered as "Google Meet call" or similar. With no meeting coming up,
there is no prepare suggestion. At most four suggestions show.

If your connections can't be read, the chat shows no suggestions rather than ones that might be wrong.

To send Vexa to a meeting, paste its link into the message box and ask, for example *Send Vexa to https://meet.google.com/abc-defg-hij*.

Pressing a suggestion acts in the same chat. A used suggestion is not offered again in that chat.
Opening **Connections** or the recorder does not use the suggestion up, and when you finish connecting, the suggestion
changes to *Check my email* or *Prepare for …* without a reload.

### Links in replies

Every page a reply names opens on click: wiki links, `.md` paths and workspace paths. A link to a page
that does not exist yet opens its path, where you can [create it](/minutes/pages#extend-or-create-a-page-with-the-agent).

**Meetings** show as a meeting chip, beside the people and companies: a microphone, the meeting's
title and its date, for example *Acme weekly sync · Fri 9 Oct*. The title is the meeting's own, so a
renamed call shows its new name; a meeting nobody named shows its platform
(*Zoom · Fri 9 Oct*). Click the chip to open the meeting, as from the Calls list. A reply gets the
chip when it links the meeting (`/?meeting=<id>`) or its notes page (`kg/entities/meeting/….md`),
including a notes page the turn wrote. The notes filename itself is never shown.

If you cannot open a meeting, its chip shows as plain text, with no title and no date.

### Choose where a chat writes

The bar above the chat shows the chat's name, then one read-only chip with its profile and scope,
for example *Work · writes to biz · reads 3* or *Writes to Personal · Reads 2 workspaces*; hover it
for the full list. It has no toggles. Say what you want in the chat — "write here", "also read
Example Bank", "no workspaces", "use my work profile" — and the agent changes it and says what
changed in one line. See [Workspace states](/core/workspaces#workspace-states-active-inactive-hidden).

- With no target set, writes go to your Personal workspace.
- **Company** (the `_global` workspace) is never offered to an everyday account, the instance
  admin's included. Signed in as the deployment's **admin account**, the bar shows **Company** alone:
  it is always the target, there is no `+`, and every chat writes there (see
  [The admin account](/minutes/workspaces#the-admin-account)).
- You can also say "work in `<workspace>`" and the agent moves the target itself.

The target is where writes go by default. It never grants a right: a workspace you cannot write is
refused as a target.

### Name, order and delete chats

A chat is named from your first message until you rename it. Your chats sit in your own sections:
**Pinned**, folders you name, **Recent** and **Hidden** (a folded folder at the bottom; hiding a
chat is moving it there). Drag a chat between them, or use **Move to…** in its menu, or ask the
agent (*"put the Acme chats in an Acme folder"*). Folders are yours alone, even on a shared chat.
Folders, renaming, the activity dot, pinning, hiding, reopening history and deleting a chat are
covered in [Manage your chats](/how-to/chat-controls). What
happens when a long turn reaches its tool-call limit is in [Long agent turns](/agent-tool-calls).

## Limits and errors

| You see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *That message did not reach your agent — nothing was lost on your side, please send it again.* | the server could not hand the message to the agent | send it again |
| *No model credentials are configured, so the agent cannot run…* | the deployment has no model provider | an operator adds one ([model credentials](/model-credentials-licensing)) |
| a red block *`<Source>` · `<kind>`* with **Retry** | a dependency failed; the turn ended with a typed fault | read the remedy line; retry |
| *the terminal could not reach the Vexa gateway* | the gateway is down or unreachable | try again in a moment |
| *not a workspace you can write* | the chosen target is read-only for you | pick a workspace where you are a contributor |
| *only the admin account may write into _global…* | **Company** belongs to the admin account | sign in as the admin account |
| *the admin account works on the company layer (_global) only…* | the admin account has no desk or other workspace | sign in with your everyday account |
| *Lost the connection to that background job…* | the stream dropped during a job | reopen the page |

Other limits: a chat id is 1–128 letters, digits, `.`, `_` or `-`; the queue lists at most 200 waiting
messages and none older than an hour; a chat shows at most 4 suggestions. A chat turn is started by a
person: an agent acting for someone cannot start one.

## How it works

### Flow

1. **Send.** The browser posts to the terminal's `/api/chat`. The terminal forwards to the gateway's
   `/agent/chat` with your API key from the sign-in cookie. The gateway resolves the key to you and
   forwards to agent-api `/api/chat` with your identity signed.
2. **Prepare.** agent-api takes the user from the signed identity, never from the body. It keeps your
   words as typed, adds any context (the target workspace, a job or flow mark) after a separator, checks
   that model credentials exist, and records the chat in your session list.
3. **Dispatch.** agent-api writes the message into the chat's inbox and starts or wakes the agent
   worker. It then relays the worker's output stream back to the browser as server-sent events. A
   reconnect resumes from the last event it saw; nothing is dispatched twice.
4. **Queue.** A message sent mid-turn goes to `/api/chat/submit`, which runs the same steps without
   streaming, on the chat's running worker (it never moves the chat to a new one). `/api/chat/pending`
   lists what is still waiting, and whether the queue is blocked and why. Given `after=<cursor>`, it
   also says how many turns the agent has started since that point of the output stream (`taken`).
5. **One view, every turn that starts on it.** The worker can take the next message while it is still
   finishing a turn's write-back, so two turns can be on the output stream at once. agent-api keeps a
   view open until every turn that started on it has ended. The browser routes each event to its turn
   by `turn_id`, so a second turn gets its own reply under the message it answers.
6. **Catch up.** When a view ends, the browser asks `/api/chat/pending` with the last event it read.
   If something is still queued, or a turn started after that event, it attaches from that event and
   shows it. It asks again when a mid-turn submission is acknowledged, because the turn in front may
   have ended while the submission was in flight.
7. **Jobs.** For `create`, `extend` and `extend_transcript`, the worker runs the act on its own thread,
   reports `job-started`, progress and `job-done` or `job-failed` on the chat's stream, and ends the
   chat turn at once so the chat can answer.

### Empty-chat suggestions

1. **Read connections.** The terminal reads your connections through the same route the Connections
   panel uses: `GET /api/connections/list` → the credential broker's `GET /api/connections`, as you.
   It returns metadata only (provider, label, status). It is re-read when the tab regains focus or a
   consent window reports back.
2. **Read the next meeting.** Only when a Google Calendar connection is `ready`, the terminal calls
   `GET /api/connections/upcoming`. Its server half asks the broker for the calendar read it already
   offers (`POST /api/connections/{id}/read`, action `calendar.events`) over a fixed window, now to
   now + 24 hours, with no input from the browser. It returns each event's title and start time only,
   and drops cancelled events. At most every five minutes while the tab is visible.
3. **Decide.** One pure function, `proposals()` in `clients/terminal/src/minutes/proposals.ts`, builds
   the row. It makes no request and asks no model. A connection counts as connected only when its
   status is `ready`. The prepare suggestion uses the soonest event that has a title and a start time
   (not all-day) inside the window (`nextMeeting`, `UPCOMING_WINDOW_MS`). Order: prepare, check email,
   connect email, connect calendar, then agent-filed items and setup; at most four.
4. **Fail closed.** Until the connections list answers, no connection suggestion shows. If it can't be
   read, the row is empty and one `suggestions_unreadable` line is logged in the browser console (once
   per page). If the calendar read fails, only the prepare suggestion is missing.
5. **Act.** A click goes through `applyProposal()`. A connect suggestion opens the Connections panel on
   that provider (and on the existing connection, when one is waiting or disconnected). The other
   suggestions send your words as a turn in this chat. The agent then uses its existing tools:
   `gmail_inbox` and `gmail_thread` for email, `calendar_events` and your transcripts and workspaces for
   meeting prep.

| Data | Single writer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Your chat list (rail) and names | agent-api | Redis, per user |
| The conversation: owner, participants, roles, transcript versions, messages the agent was not asked to answer | agent-api | the instance's Postgres (`agent_session*` tables) and objects under `sessions/` in the install's S3 store ([Shared chats](/minutes/shared-chats#where-a-conversation-lives)) |
| Rail order | agent-api | Redis, per user |
| Folders, pins and hides | agent-api | the session store, per user (Redis marks without the store) |
| Chat inbox | agent-api (every entry signed) | the chat unit's input stream |
| Turn output and job events | the agent worker | the chat unit's output stream; agent-api only relays it |
| Inbox position | the agent worker | the chat unit's cursor |
| Job records | the agent worker | the chat's working folder |
| Suggestions filed by agents | agent-api | `.vexa/proposals.json` in your Personal workspace |
| Connection status | credential broker | the broker's database; the terminal only reads it |
| The suggestion row and the next-meeting title | nobody: derived in the browser on each render and never stored | — |
| Open tabs, view, unsent copies | your browser | `localStorage` |

### Meeting chips

1. A meeting link (`?meeting=<id>` or `?meeting=<platform>/<native id>`) is looked up in your
   meetings list, the same `GET /api/meetings` list the Calls list shows. Found: the chip. Not
   found: plain text.
2. A notes-page link goes to `POST /api/links/resolve`. agent-api finds the page in the workspace
   you are reading, then on your desk. It reads the meeting id from the page's `meeting:` field and
   asks meeting-api for that meeting as you. It answers `kind: "meeting"` with the title (`data.title`)
   and time, or `not-yours` with no title. A page with no `meeting:` field keeps its own title.
3. The agent is told to name meetings by their link, never by the notes filename.

### Configuration

`GATEWAY_URL` on the terminal server. The job and tool-budget limits are worker settings
(`VEXA_AGENT_JOB_MAX_TOOL_CALLS` and related, see `core/agent/llm/JOBS.md`).

The fast intent router is the `intent_router` feature (on with `VEXA_FEATURES=all`, off in a release
until switched on; `features: {intent_router: off}` in `_global/POLICIES.md` turns it off). Rules decide
first. Only a short message that names a call, open, invite or schedule word and fits no rule is put to
a small model (`model_intent_router`, else `model_home`), which must answer within 0.7 s with confidence
0.8 or more; its spend is counted as usage kind `route`. Every routed message leaves a receipt (route,
confidence, latency, source, executed or fell through): an `intent_route` log line, an entry in the
session's own log (the newest 50, never shown as a chat line or heard by the agent), and a `route` event
at the head of the chat's stream.

### Security

**Who can do what.** Only you can post into a chat of your own. In a [shared chat](/minutes/shared-chats) its owner and members post too; viewers only read. The chat's target is a default, never a grant:
writes are still checked against your rights in that workspace, and an agent acting for you is held to
the workspaces of the chat that dispatched it. Only the admin account can target the company workspace,
and the admin account can target nothing else.

**Protections.**

- The user comes from the gateway-signed identity; an unsigned or missing identity is refused, and a
  `subject` in the body is ignored.
- Unknown request fields are refused, so no field can widen a request.
- A message composed by Vexa or dispatched by a flow is marked, and never shown as your words.
- An agent acting for someone cannot start a chat turn.
- Job events carry their chat's id, so one chat's job never shows in another.
- Suggestions read only your own desk and connections. The connections list returns metadata
  (provider, label, status), never a token or any mail content, and only the signed-in person's rows.
- The next-meeting read is your own calendar, through the broker, as you, over a fixed window the
  browser cannot change. Only titles and start times reach the browser; descriptions, attendees,
  locations and links do not. Nothing is stored by the terminal; the broker keeps its usual audit row
  for the read (who, which connection, when, outcome), never the content.
- Connecting still happens in the Connections panel, on your click. A suggestion never reads mail
  itself: *Check my email* is your message to the agent, which reads with its own audited tools.
- A meeting chip's title and date come only from meetings you can open: your meetings list, or
  meeting-api's access check (owner, shared with you, or a member of its workspace). Holding a copy
  of someone's notes page does not reveal the meeting's name.
