---
title: "Calls"
description: "Start an audio or video call from a shared chat in Minutes. The call is transcribed per speaker, the chat's agent follows it, and its owner reviews what it changes."
---

A call is a conversation you start from a chat in Minutes, held on your deployment's own LiveKit
media server. Everyone in the chat can join. The call is transcribed as people speak, and each
person's words are attributed to them, because each person sends their own audio track. The chat
stays beside the call: the agent follows the live transcript and answers when someone mentions it.
When the call ends, it is kept as a meeting, like any other meeting.

Calls are an optional module. A deployment without the LiveKit server shows no Call button.

## What you can do

### Start or join a call

1. Open a chat that has other people in it. Use **People** beside the chat's name to add them.
2. Press **Call**. If a call is already running in this chat, the button reads **Join call · N**,
   where N is the number of people in the call.
3. The browser asks for the microphone the first time. The call opens as one compact bar at the top of
   the conversation. The bar stays there while you move between chats.

The bar shows the call's name, how long it has run, who is in it (a ring lights on whoever speaks),
and a small Vexa mark while the call is transcribed. Its icon buttons, each with a tooltip:

- the microphone (mute and unmute);
- the camera (its tiles open under the bar while any camera or shared screen is on);
- screen share;
- **…**: your microphone, camera and speaker, **Notes and transcript** (the meeting's own view),
  and **Add people** (mention them in the chat);
- the red phone icon: leave the call.

A viewer of the chat joins the call to listen. A viewer cannot speak.

### The transcription notice

Before joining, every participant sees one quiet line, *This call is transcribed · everyone in it
sees this*, and presses **Join**. Until they do, the dock does not connect to the room and the
microphone stays off; **Cancel** leaves without connecting. In the call, the Vexa mark in the bar
says the call is transcribed (its tooltip says so in words). Vexa's transcriber is not a person in the
call and not a bot you manage: it starts with the call and ends with it, and there is no Stop bot for
a call. The notice comes from the room itself, so every client shows the same thing.

Calls are always transcribed unless the administrator sets `CALL_TRANSCRIPTION_SWITCHABLE=true`.
When that setting is on, a call in an **incognito** chat (its profile chip) is started untranscribed,
and so can any call through the API (`POST /bots {"platform":"vexa_call", "transcribe": false}`). An
untranscribed call starts **no transcriber at all**: nothing joins the room to capture audio, no
meeting is made, and no transcript or notes exist afterwards; the bar says *not transcribed*. With
the setting off, starting a call in an incognito chat is refused with a message saying that calls here
are always transcribed.

### Talk to the agent during the call

The chat is the call's shared conversation. Everyone in the call and the agent are in it. The agent
reads the live transcript and answers when you mention it (`@vexa`), as in any chat with more than one
person. It uses only what every participant can read.

### Your private side chat

**…** → **Notes and transcript** opens the call's meeting: its note, the transcript in the sidebar's
drawer, and your own chat about it, with your agent and your workspaces. Nobody else sees that chat.

To say something to the room in writing, write in the shared chat itself: a shared chat is one
stream. Nothing crosses from your private chat on its own.

### Background blur

Blur what is behind you with the **Blur** button on your own video tile (it shows when you point at
the tile, and always on a touch screen), in the call bar's **…**, or before you join. Each press goes
**off → light → strong → off**. The choice is yours and is kept in this browser: it applies every time
your camera starts.

It runs in your browser (MediaPipe selfie segmentation through `@livekit/track-processors`), so the
others receive the blurred picture. Its runtime and model are served by your deployment at
`/mediapipe/`, never from a third-party CDN. In a browser that cannot run it, or on a device that
cannot keep up, blur turns itself off with one short notice and your camera carries on unprocessed.

### Show a page to everyone

In a call, open a page of a shared workspace and choose **…** → **Show to everyone in the call**, or
ask the agent ("show the sponsor plan to everyone", "highlight the budget"). The page opens in every
participant's panel, and a line under the call bar says *Shown by Dmitry · Sponsor plan*.

- **Following.** Everyone follows the person who showed it: their scroll position, and whatever they
  select, which is highlighted for everyone and scrolled into view. **Stop following** lets you browse
  on your own; **Follow** brings you back. A highlight fades after a few seconds unless it is pinned.
- **Access.** Only a page that every person in the chat can already read is shown. If someone cannot,
  the show is refused and you are offered to share the workspace with them first (they are added as
  contributors when you confirm). A private page is never sent.
- **What is kept.** Nothing but one line in the chat: *Dmitry showed “Sponsor plan” · 14:05*, naming the
  page. The show itself travels over the call's LiveKit data channel and is not stored.

This is switched by the `call_show` feature (`VEXA_FEATURES`, or `features:` in `_global/POLICIES.md`).

### Review after the call

After the call, the post-meeting run proposes changes:

- the meeting's report, as a page in the owner's Personal workspace;
- one proposal for each commitment the report names.

**Review · N** appears beside the chat's name while proposals are waiting.

The person who started the call (the meeting's owner) can accept a proposal, edit it before accepting,
or reject it. Other participants can see the list, but cannot decide anything on it.

Nothing lands in a workspace until the owner accepts it. An accepted page is written to its workspace
and committed with the owner as its author. The owner's own write rights apply.

An administrator can set the policy `post_meeting_auto_accept: on` in the organisation's
`POLICIES.md` (`_global`). A workspace can also set it in its own `POLICIES.md`, and the workspace's
setting wins over the organisation's, whether it is `on` or `off`. With the policy on, each proposed
change lands at once, in the owner's name and with the owner's rights. The default is `off`.

### When a call ends

A call ends when the last person in it presses Leave: a few seconds later the transcriber leaves and
the meeting completes. If the last person's connection drops instead, the call waits
`CALL_EMPTY_GRACE_SEC` (30 seconds by default) for them to come back; joining again within that time
continues the same call and the same meeting. It also ends if nobody joins within `CALL_FIRST_JOIN_TIMEOUT_SEC` (30 seconds by
default: a call started and cancelled before anyone joined does not keep its transcriber waiting), or when it reaches `CALL_MAX_DURATION_SEC` (four hours by default).

When the call ends, the meeting completes like any meeting. Its transcript, notes, search, sharing and
export all work on it. Everyone who joined the call can read its transcript.

Participants read the transcript through a share that Minutes makes when the call starts, and that
share lasts six hours. If you raise `CALL_MAX_DURATION_SEC` above six hours (`21600`), people who
join after the sixth hour cannot redeem it, and their join fails.

## How it works

### Flow

1. **Start.** The terminal posts `POST /api/bots {"platform": "vexa_call", "session_id": "s-…"}`.
   meeting-api:
   - creates a LiveKit room named `call-…`, with metadata that holds the transcription notice, the
     owner and the session;
   - makes the meeting row, with platform `vexa_call` and the room name as its id;
   - spawns the listener;
   - writes the meeting row's id into the room's metadata.

   Rooms are never created by joining: the server runs with `room.auto_create: false`.
2. **Bind.** The terminal mints a transcript share for the meeting
   (`POST /api/meetings/{id}/share`). It then posts the room, the meeting and the share to agent-api
   (`POST /api/sessions/{s}/call`). agent-api reads the room's metadata back from the LiveKit server,
   and binds the call to the session only if the metadata names the caller, this session and this
   meeting.
3. **Join.** Each participant asks agent-api for a room token (`POST /api/sessions/{s}/call/token`).
   agent-api mints the token only for a participant of the session, and only for the session's
   room. The token is valid for ten minutes, which covers joining and reconnecting. A viewer's token
   can subscribe but not publish.

   Joining also redeems the call's transcript share as the participant, so they can read the
   transcript. The browser connects to the LiveKit server with
   [LiveKit's React components](https://github.com/livekit/components-js). Capture uses the browser's
   own echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control. Audio is sent as Opus with DTX and
   redundancy.
4. **Capture.** The listener is the meeting bot without a browser. It joins the room hidden and can
   only subscribe, using `@livekit/rtc-node`. It subscribes to every audio track a participant
   publishes and reads it as mono 16 kHz audio, on that participant's own channel, named after them. That channel is fed into the same
   per-speaker lane, speech-to-text service and transcript sinks that a meeting and a voice note use.
   Digital silence (a muted or quiet track) is not fed, so the lane sees the gaps between speech.
5. **Follow.** A turn in the shared chat is grounded in the call's live transcript. The access check
   still applies: a participant who has not joined the call grounds nothing.
6. **End.** When the last participant leaves (LiveKit reports a client-initiated disconnect), the
   listener waits three seconds; when the last connection is lost instead, it waits the grace period.
   Then it leaves, flushes the last words and reports the meeting `completed`. agent-api sees that the room is gone and ends the
   session's call.

### Routes

| Route | Service | Who may call it |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /bots {"platform":"vexa_call"}` | meeting-api | a person with the `bot` scope; the caller becomes the owner |
| `GET /agent/sessions/{s}/call` | agent-api | any participant of the session |
| `POST /agent/sessions/{s}/call` | agent-api | an owner or member; only for their own room |
| `POST /agent/sessions/{s}/call/token` | agent-api | any participant; a viewer's token cannot publish |
| `POST /agent/sessions/{s}/messages` | agent-api | an owner or member (a line in the shared chat) |
| `GET /agent/sessions/{s}/proposals` | agent-api | any participant |
| `POST /agent/sessions/{s}/proposals` | agent-api | the internal tier (the post-meeting run) |
| `POST /agent/sessions/{s}/proposals/{id}/accept`, `…/reject` | agent-api | the meeting's owner |

Without a LiveKit server, the call routes answer `503 calls_not_deployed`, and `GET …/call` says
`calls: false`.

### Who writes what

| Data | Single writer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The room and its metadata (the notice, owner, session and meeting) | meeting-api | LiveKit server |
| The meeting row and its status | meeting-api | Postgres |
| Transcript segments | the listener, then meeting-api | the transcript stream in Redis, then Postgres |
| The session's call, and the proposals with their decisions | agent-api | `agent_session_call` and `agent_session_proposal` tables |
| An accepted page | agent-api, as the owner | the workspace's git, authored by the owner |

### Network requirements

Calls are real-time media. Plan the network before you turn them on:

| Port | Protocol | Who reaches it | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7880 | TCP (WebSocket) | browsers, through your TLS proxy as `wss://`; meeting-api, agent-api and the listener inside the deployment | signalling and the room service |
| 50000–50100 (configurable) | UDP | browsers | media |
| 7881 | TCP | browsers on networks that block UDP | ICE over TCP |
| 3478 | UDP | browsers on networks that allow UDP but block the media range | the embedded TURN server, when enabled |
| 5349 (configurable) | TCP (TLS) | browsers on networks that block UDP or allow only TLS | TURN over TLS, when enabled |

- Browsers need a `wss://` address (`LIVEKIT_URL`). Put the server behind the same TLS proxy as the
  terminal, or give it its own name.
- The UDP range must be open in the firewall. The server advertises the address browsers should use:
  `LIVEKIT_USE_EXTERNAL_IP=true` discovers it through STUN, or you can set `LIVEKIT_NODE_IP`.
- Each person in a call uses up to two ports of the UDP range (one connection to send, one to
  receive). The default range of 101 ports therefore carries about 50 people at once across all
  calls. Widen the range for more, and open the wider range in the firewall.
- Restrictive networks need a relay. Pick by what the network allows:
  - **UDP allowed, but not the media range:** the embedded TURN server on 3478/udp
    (`LIVEKIT_TURN_ENABLED=true` and `LIVEKIT_TURN_DOMAIN`).
  - **No UDP at all:** TURN over TLS. Give the server a DNS name that points at the LiveKit host
    (`LIVEKIT_TURN_DOMAIN`, for example `turn.example.com`) and a certificate for that name, then set
    the TLS port (5349 by default; see [Configuration](#configuration) for the keys). Browsers then
    relay media inside TLS on that port. ICE over TCP on 7881 is the fallback when no relay is set up.
  - **Only 443 outbound:** TURN over TLS must answer on 443 itself. On Compose, set
    `LIVEKIT_TURN_TLS_PORT=443` on a host where nothing else holds 443. The Helm chart does not cover
    this case: its server runs without the right to open ports below 1024. There, run the LiveKit
    server yourself with TURN on 443, as described in
    [LiveKit's deployment guide](https://docs.livekit.io/home/self-hosting/deployment/).
- One server carries many small audio calls. Video needs more bandwidth and CPU, so size the host
  for the number of video calls you expect at once.

### Configuration

The services' keys:

| Key | Service | Default | Compose (`.env`) | Helm value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LIVEKIT_URL` | meeting-api, agent-api | — | `LIVEKIT_URL` | `livekit.url` | the server's public `wss://` address; unset means no calls |
| `LIVEKIT_INTERNAL_URL` | meeting-api, agent-api | `LIVEKIT_URL` | `LIVEKIT_INTERNAL_URL` | set by the chart | the server inside the deployment, for example `ws://livekit:7880` |
| `LIVEKIT_API_KEY` | meeting-api, agent-api | — | `LIVEKIT_API_KEY` | `livekit.apiKey` | the key that signs room tokens |
| `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET` | meeting-api, agent-api | — | `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET` | generated into the chart's Secret | **secret**, at least 32 characters |
| `CALL_MAX_DURATION_SEC` | meeting-api | `14400` | `CALL_MAX_DURATION_SEC` | `meetingApi.calls.maxDurationSeconds` | the longest call |
| `CALL_EMPTY_GRACE_SEC` | meeting-api | `30` | `CALL_EMPTY_GRACE_SEC` | `meetingApi.calls.emptyGraceSeconds` | how long an emptied room waits before the call ends |
| `CALL_FIRST_JOIN_TIMEOUT_SEC` | meeting-api | `30` | `CALL_FIRST_JOIN_TIMEOUT_SEC` | `meetingApi.calls.firstJoinTimeoutSeconds` | how long a new call waits for its first participant |
| `CALL_MAX_PARTICIPANTS` | meeting-api | `50` | `CALL_MAX_PARTICIPANTS` | `meetingApi.calls.maxParticipants` | the most people one room admits |
| `CALL_TRANSCRIPTION_SWITCHABLE` | meeting-api | `false` | `CALL_TRANSCRIPTION_SWITCHABLE` | `meetingApi.calls.transcriptionSwitchable` | whether a call may be started untranscribed through the API |

The LiveKit server's own settings:

| Compose (`.env`) | Helm value | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LIVEKIT_SIGNAL_BIND`, `LIVEKIT_SIGNAL_PORT` | `livekit.bindAddresses`, `livekit.service.port` | Compose `127.0.0.1:7880`; Helm the pod's IP, port `7880` | where signalling listens; keep it behind your TLS proxy |
| `LIVEKIT_UDP_PORT_START`, `LIVEKIT_UDP_PORT_END` | `livekit.udpPortStart`, `livekit.udpPortEnd` | `50000`, `50100` | the media range |
| `LIVEKIT_USE_EXTERNAL_IP`, `LIVEKIT_NODE_IP` | `livekit.useExternalIp`, `livekit.nodeIp` | `false` (Helm `true`), none | the address advertised to browsers |
| `LIVEKIT_TURN_ENABLED`, `LIVEKIT_TURN_DOMAIN` | `livekit.turn.enabled`, `livekit.turn.domain` | off, none | the embedded TURN server on 3478/udp, and the DNS name browsers dial for it |
| `LIVEKIT_TURN_TLS_PORT` | `livekit.turn.tlsPort` | off (Helm `0`) | TURN over TLS on this TCP port (5349 recommended; Helm requires a port above 1024) |
| `LIVEKIT_TURN_CERT_FILE`, `LIVEKIT_TURN_KEY_FILE` | `livekit.turn.tlsSecretName` | none | the certificate and key for `LIVEKIT_TURN_DOMAIN`: host paths of PEM files on Compose, a `kubernetes.io/tls` Secret on Helm |

TURN over TLS needs TURN enabled, a domain and a certificate. Without the certificate the LiveKit
server does not start, and Helm refuses to render.

Set them:

- **Compose:** the `calls` profile (`docker compose --profile calls up -d livekit`). Put the keys in
  `deploy/compose/.env` and apply them with `docker compose up -d meeting-api agent-api` and, for the
  server's own settings, `docker compose --profile calls up -d livekit`. A `restart` does not re-read
  `.env`. See [Deployment](/deployment#calls-optional).
- **Helm:** set `livekit.enabled: true` and `livekit.url`, and the values above. The chart generates
  the secret. See [Kubernetes](/deployment-kubernetes).
- **Lite:** not supported. Lite runs no LiveKit server, so it has no calls.

## Why it complies

### Architecture

| Rule | How this design meets it | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P2** contracts | a call is the sealed `invocation.v1` / `api.v1` / `ws.v1` platform value `vexa_call`; the listener's room is the `invocation.v1` `Call` block | `gate:schema`, `gate:contract-version` |
| **P3** one owner per domain (ADR-0037) | the meeting is meeting-api's, which creates the room and listener; the session is agent-api's, which decides who joins; neither calls the other to decide | meeting-api `test_call_spawn.py`, agent-api `test_session_calls.py` |
| **P7** disposable workers | the listener is the ordinary meeting bot without its browser: it boots, joins, emits and dies | bot `call.test.ts` |
| **P11** we operate media, we do not write it | the media server is unmodified LiveKit (Apache-2.0), pinned by digest | `gate:image-licenses` |
| **P14** config is a contract | every service key above is declared in `config.v1` and set on Compose and Helm, the two surfaces that carry calls | `gate:config-contract` |
| **P20** authorize every access | room tokens are minted only for session participants, for one room, for ten minutes; joining never creates a room | the deny tests below |
| **P22** guaranteed teardown | an emptied room, a room nobody joined, or the cap ends the call; the room expires on the server too | bot `call.test.ts` |

### Security

**Who can do what.**

- Only a participant of the chat gets a token for its call. The token is valid only for that call's
  room, for ten minutes, and a viewer's token cannot publish.
- Only the person who started a call can bind it to a chat. Only that person can accept what it
  proposes.
- The listener cannot publish anything. It joins as a hidden LiveKit participant; the dock shows it as
  the Vexa mark and the notice tells everyone that it is there.
- Nobody's microphone is captured before they have seen the notice and pressed **Join**.

**Protections.**

- The API secret signs every token and is held only by:
  - the LiveKit server;
  - meeting-api;
  - agent-api.

  On Compose, the runtime loads the whole `.env`, so its environment blanks `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET`.

  Browsers receive short-lived tokens only.
- Room metadata is written only by meeting-api. agent-api trusts a call's owner, session and meeting
  only as they appear in metadata it reads from the server itself.
- The listener's token is carried in its invocation. It is never stored on the meeting row and never
  logged.
- An accepted change is written with the owner's own rights, under the delegation ceiling, and
  committed in the owner's name.

**Deny tests.**

| Test | Proves |
|---|---|
| `core/agent/tests/test_session_calls.py` · `test_a_call_binds_only_with_matching_room_metadata` | nobody binds another person's room, or a room for another chat |
| `core/agent/tests/test_session_calls.py` · `test_a_participant_gets_a_short_lived_token_for_this_room_only` | non-participants get nothing; viewers cannot publish; the token names one room and expires in ten minutes |
| `core/agent/tests/test_session_calls.py` · `test_the_meeting_owner_reviews_proposals_and_an_accepted_page_is_committed_as_them` | only the owner decides, nothing lands before acceptance, the commit is the owner's |
| `core/meetings/services/meeting-api/tests/test_call_spawn.py` · `test_transcription_cannot_be_turned_off_unless_the_deployment_allows_it` | the notice cannot be switched off by a caller |
| `core/meetings/services/meeting-api/tests/test_call_livekit_token.py` | the listener is hidden and cannot publish; admin tokens name one room |
| `clients/terminal/src/minutes/__tests__/callConsent.test.tsx` | no room connection and no microphone before **Join**; the transcriber is listed in the call |
| `core/meetings/services/meeting-api/tests/test_call_spawn.py` · `test_a_call_over_the_bot_limit_leaves_no_room_behind` | a call refused by the bot limit or the service authority, or whose listener fails to start, leaves no room |

### End to end

`core/meetings/eval/call-e2e/run.sh` starts a throwaway LiveKit server, the branch's meeting-api and
gateway, and the listener. Two headless participants publish synthetic speech, one after the other,
and a real speech-to-text service transcribes it.

On 2026-10-12, the results were:

| Speaker | Published | Received by the listener | Transcribed | Text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ada | 17.31 s | 17.69 s | 17.69 s | every sentence, in order |
| Grace | 14.70 s | 13.27 s | 13.27 s | every sentence, in order |

- Each speaker's words were attributed to that speaker.
- The meeting `completed` 8 seconds after the last person left.
- Grace's received and transcribed figures are lower than her published figure because digital
  silence between her sentences is not fed to the lane.
