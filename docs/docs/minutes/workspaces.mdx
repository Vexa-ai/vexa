A workspace is a shared folder of pages with a roster. Your **personal workspace** is yours; a **shared workspace**
has members; the **company** workspace (`_global`) is read by everyone and written only by the
deployment's **admin account**. This page covers the admin account, the front page of a workspace, how people join one, links into it,
attaching an existing repository, and the **Connections** page where you connect your own accounts.

A workspace holds knowledge: pages, files and their history. It holds no chat. Every conversation is
kept in the session store, apart from all workspaces, your private `_system` included, so sharing a
workspace never shares a chat and sharing a chat never shares a workspace
([Shared chats](/minutes/shared-chats)).

## What you can do

### Read a workspace's front page

Open a workspace's `README.md` (or a `/w/<workspace>` link) and its header shows:

- **What it is:** *Your personal workspace*, *Shared workspace* or *Company layer*, with its title.
- **Who is in it:** for a shared workspace, up to two names with *you* first ("you, … and 2 more");
  for your personal workspace, whether agents read it for your meetings; for the company workspace, who reads and
  who writes it. A reader who may not see the roster sees *you and the other members*.
- **What is in it:** the page count, and what the workspace is bound to (a meeting series, or where it
  came from).
- **What changed last:** *… changed the … page …*, with a link when one page changed, or *Nothing
  written here yet*.

The buttons depend on your role:

| You are | Buttons |
|---|---|
| a member who is not an owner | **History** only |
| owner of a shared workspace | **Add a member**, **Connect a repo** (or **Sync**), **History** |
| owner of your personal workspace | **Connect a repo** (or **Sync**), **History** |
| the admin account, on the company workspace | **Set up policies**, **History** |

Every button except History starts a conversation in the chat; none opens a form.

**History** opens *People*, *Repo* and *History* (and *Meetings* for a shared workspace):

- **People** lists members and roles. Owners can **Change role**, **Remove** and **Add a member…**.
  Members who are not contributors or owners see their own role only.
- **Repo** shows the attached remote, branch, and how far ahead or behind it is. Owners get
  **Sync now**, **Pull**, **Push** and **Detach**; Pull, Push and Detach ask you to confirm. Detaching
  stops syncing and leaves the files as they are.
- **History** lists commits ten at a time, for the workspace or *This page only*.

### Create and share a workspace from the chat

Say it in one sentence: *"create a workspace called eugenia - dmitry and share it with
ana@example.com"*. The agent creates it, invites the address and confirms in one line.

- The new workspace is addressed by its name: "eugenia - dmitry" becomes `eugenia-dmitry`
  (`eugenia-dmitry-2` if you already have one).
- It starts private. The first invite makes it shared, with you as owner.
- An invite with no role makes the person a **contributor**. Say "as reader" or "as owner" to choose,
  or change it afterwards.
- Name any workspace the way the Files pane shows it ("Eugenia - Dmitry", "biz", "Personal"). If two
  workspaces answer one name, the agent asks which.
- If two of your workspaces share a name and one is empty, the agent offers to delete the empty one,
  and deletes it only after you say yes.

### Forget something

Say *"delete that document and everything from it"* or *"forget this"*. The agent tells you in one
line what will be removed, and removes it only after you say yes:

- The files leave the workspace and its git history. History is rewritten and old copies are
  pruned, so no commit still holds them.
- Text you name, and the document's own identifiers, are replaced with `[removed]` in every
  version of every page and in the stored transcripts of your chats. The turns themselves stay.
- They are dropped from your recently-opened list.

The agent then says what was removed and what could not be. Copies already sent by email, or
downloaded, are out of reach. A repository the workspace syncs with keeps its copy until you agree
to a force-push. Your private `_system` workspace keeps an audit line: what kind of item, how many
files and commits, who asked. It never keeps the content.

Files you drop into a chat stay in the chat's drawer (`uploads/`) and are never committed to the
workspace's history. A page never holds a government id, a passport, card or bank account number,
or a credential: a write carrying one is refused, and such values are removed before a turn
commits.

### Choose where new chats write

A chat's header says where it writes ("Writes to Personal", "Writes to Biz"). To have every new chat
start in one workspace, open that menu in a chat that writes there and pick **Make default for new
chats**. The menu then reads **Default for new chats**; elsewhere it shows the current default beside
the entry. Pick it from a chat that writes to Personal to go back to Personal.

- A new chat starts on the default: the workspace is added to the chat and becomes where it writes.
- Chats that already exist keep where they write.
- The default is checked again for every new chat. If you can no longer write there (you were
  removed, the workspace was deleted, or your role is now reader), the new chat writes to Personal
  and the workspace is not named.
- The default is kept on the server, per person, so it follows you to every device.

### The admin account

The company workspace has exactly one writer: the deployment's **admin account**. It is a dedicated
account, not anybody's everyday one. It writes `_global` — its pages, `POLICIES.md` and the flows —
and it does nothing else: it has no desk, no shared workspaces and no meetings. Every other account,
including the person who administers Settings, reads `_global` and never writes it.

**Get one (operator).**

1. Pick a fresh address that has never signed in to this deployment — for example
   `admin@example.com`, or a plus-address such as `you+admin@example.com`.
2. Set it as `VEXA_ADMIN_ACCOUNT` on admin-api (compose `.env`, Helm `adminApi.adminAccount`, or
   `docker run -e VEXA_ADMIN_ACCOUNT=…` for Lite) and restart admin-api.
3. Sign in with that address, the same way anyone signs in (the emailed link, or Google or Microsoft
   for that address). The address is admitted even on a closed instance.

An address that already belongs to an everyday account is **never** turned into the admin account;
admin-api says so in its log at boot. Pick another address. Removing the address from
`VEXA_ADMIN_ACCOUNT` takes the role away at the next restart. With the key empty there is no admin
account and nobody writes `_global`.

**What you see, signed in as the admin account.** The terminal shows one workspace: **Company**. Every
chat writes there; there is no desk, no `+` menu, and no meetings list to fill. The first sign-in
opens the company setup; after that an empty chat offers four areas — **Flows & mail**,
**Templates**, **Friction** and **Workspace**. [Admin account](/minutes/admin-account) covers each.
Sign out and back in with your everyday account for everything else.

### Add a member

1. Press **Add a member** (or ask in the chat).
2. The agent asks for their email address and a role, and confirms in one sentence:
   - **owner** — writes the workspace and can add or remove members;
   - **contributor** — writes the workspace;
   - **reader** — reads it and does not write it.
3. The agent creates an invite link for that address. If the person has no account here, the link is
   emailed to them; otherwise the chat gives you the link to pass on.

Changing a role and removing someone work the same way, from **People** or in the chat. A workspace
always keeps at least one owner.

### Join from an invite

1. Open the link (`/join?i=…`). The page says who invited you, to which workspace, and as what.
2. Sign in with Google, Microsoft or an emailed sign-in link. If the invite names one address, that
   address is filled in and cannot be changed.
3. You are added and land on the workspace's front page. If you were already signed in, this happens
   at once.

An invite link works for **one person, for seven days**, and only for the address it names. Invites made
by the agent during a chat turn stay valid after the turn ends. Once an invite has expired, been used
or been withdrawn, its link says only that; it no longer shows the workspace, who sent it, or the
address it was for.

### Share a workspace by link

Ask in the chat: *"make a link to this workspace for up to 5 people"*. The agent calls
`workspace_invite_link_create` and gives you a link like `https://<your app>/join/<token>`, once.

- Anyone who opens it signs in (Google, Microsoft or an emailed link) and joins as a **contributor**
  (or a **reader**, if you said so). Someone already in it spends no use.
- It works for the number of people you set (default 10) until it expires (default 7 days) or you
  revoke it (*"stop that link"*, `workspace_invite_link_revoke`). `workspace_invite_links` lists your
  links with how many people used each; a link's address is shown only when it is made.
- Each time someone joins, you get a notification: *"Bea joined “DNA” via your link"*.
- People you share a workspace with appear when you type **@** and in people search.
- To invite people to a meeting, ask the agent to create the calendar event: it adds a Google Meet
  link, the attendees and `info@` (Vexa), puts the workspace link in the description, and asks Google
  to send the invitations. The link in the event is how the meeting is filed into that workspace.

A used-up, expired or revoked link says so, and nothing more.

### Link to a page

Every workspace has a permanent id. A link `/w/<id>/<path>` (or `/w/<name>/<path>`) opens that page in
Minutes; with no path it opens the front page. Links made with the id keep working after the workspace
is renamed. Reloading the page reopens the same page. Only the workspace's owner, or an administrator,
can rename it.

### Attach an existing repository

Choose **Attach existing repo…** in the chat bar's **+** menu, **Attach a repo…** on the front page, or
**Attach repository as a workspace** under Connections → Git repositories.

1. Enter the **Repository** address (for example `git@github.com:<org>/<repo>.git`) and the **Branch**
   (`main` by default). The repository always becomes a **new workspace**; your personal workspace and other
   workspaces are unchanged.
2. Press **Attach**. Large repositories can take several minutes; the dialog shows progress.
3. When it is done (*Cloned … into …*), press **Open workspace**.

Access uses your saved token for the repository's git provider, or a **deploy key**: under Connections,
press **Use this deploy key**, then **Add it on …** (the provider's deploy-key page) and add the public key to the repository *with write access*. Vexa
generates the key; you never paste a private key, and the private half never leaves the server.

### Repositories on GitLab or another self-hosted server

A repository on your company's own git server attaches the same way, once your operator has added
that server as a **git provider** ([Use a self-hosted GitLab](/minutes/self-hosted-gitlab)).

**The address can be nested.** GitLab keeps projects in groups and subgroups, so paste the full address:
`https://git.example.com/platform/tools/notes.git` or `git@git.example.com:platform/tools/notes.git`.
Any depth up to 22 segments (a group, 20 subgroups and the project) is accepted. On a GitHub provider
the address is still exactly `owner/repo`.

**The credential.** Use one of these:

- **A deploy key** (recommended). Paste the `git@…` address, press **Use this deploy key**, and add the
  public key in the project under *Settings → Repository → Deploy keys* with **Grant write
  permissions**. For a configured provider, Vexa links you straight to that page.
- **Your saved token for that provider**, saved once in Connections → Git. Each provider has its own
  token, and a saved token is only ever sent to its own provider.
- **An access token typed for this one call.** A GitLab personal, project or group access token with
  `read_repository` (and `write_repository` to push). Type it as `oauth2:<token>`, or as
  `<username>:<token>` for a server that wants a named account; a bare token works on a configured
  provider.

**Publishing a new repository to GitLab.** Ask the chat, or use **Publish to …** in the workspace's
Repository section: pick the provider and type the group (`platform/tools`, nested groups included).
The project is created there and the workspace pushed. Details, token scopes and errors:
[Use a self-hosted GitLab](/minutes/self-hosted-gitlab).

### Sync with the repository

Press **Sync** on a workspace's front page, or ask the chat ("is this ahead of the repository?"). The
agent first calls `workspace_status`, which fetches from the repository and tells you what is **ahead**
(only here) and **behind** (only on the remote), with the commits on each side and any uncommitted files. Looking
changes nothing in the workspace. The agent then asks you before it pulls (`workspace_pull`) or pushes
(`workspace_push`).

### Connect your accounts

Open **Connections** from your account menu (bottom of the rail). An agent can also ask you to connect
something, which opens the same page.

| Card | Lets your agents |
|---|---|
| **Git repositories** | save a token for each git provider (GitHub, your GitLab), or attach a repository |
| **Gmail** | read email and save drafts (Minutes does not send mail) |
| **Google Calendar** | read calendar events |
| **Custom secret** | use a secret, optionally against one HTTPS address you name |

- You can connect several accounts of one kind: give each a **Connection label** ("Personal",
  "Work") and press **Add another … account**.
- Google consent opens in a pop-up. Allow pop-ups for Minutes.
- A custom secret names the exact HTTPS address, the header, the value format and the one method it may
  be used with. You confirm the destination host by typing it; an unknown host is flagged.
- **Disconnect** stops use and keeps the connection; **Delete** removes it (audit history is kept).

How an operator runs the credential broker behind this page is in [Connections](/connections).

## Limits and errors

| You see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *This invite has expired.* · *…has already been used.* · *…was withdrawn.* | invites last seven days and work once | ask for a new one |
| *This invite was sent to a different address…* | you signed in with another address | sign in with the invited address |
| *This invite link is not valid.* | the link does not match any invite | ask for a new one |
| *This invite could not be checked just now.* | the service did not answer | try again |
| an owner-only refusal from the agent | only owners add, remove or change members | ask an owner |
| *cannot remove the last owner* | a workspace needs an owner | add another owner first |
| *That page is in …, a workspace you do not have access to.* · *That link points at a workspace that is no longer here.* | a `/w/` link you cannot open; Minutes never shows your personal workspace instead | ask for access |
| *Repository access failed. Check the repository URL and credential.* | the clone was refused | check the address, token or deploy key |
| *That is not a repository. Use https://github.com/owner/repo, …* | the address has an empty segment, `.`/`..`, a GitLab page path (`/-/…`), more than 22 segments, or more than `owner/repo` on `github.com` | paste the project's clone address |
| *Vexa can create a repository on this deployment's git providers: …* (`422`) | publish was asked to create on a provider that is not configured | use a listed provider, or create it there and publish with `remote_url` |
| *… rejected the token (HTTP 401)* (`422`, `token_rejected`) | the provider refused the token | save a new token for that provider in Connections → Git |
| *GitLab on … rejected the token (HTTP 401)* · *GitLab group … was not found, or the token cannot see it* | the token lacks the `api` scope, or the group path is wrong | check the token's scopes and the `org` group path |
| *Import interrupted. Check the workspace before retrying.* | the import stopped part-way | check what arrived, then retry |
| *Allow popups for Minutes, then try again.* · *Authorization was not completed.* | Google consent did not finish | allow pop-ups and retry |
| *Connections unavailable. Please try again.* | the credential service did not answer | try again; an operator checks the broker |
| *only the admin account may edit _global* (or *write…*, *reset…*, *accept…*) | you are on an everyday account | sign in as the admin account |
| *the admin account works on the company layer (_global) only…* | the admin account has no desk, workspaces or meetings | sign in with your everyday account |
| the agent says the workspace has **no git home** (`no_remote`) | nothing is attached or published | attach or publish a repository |
| the agent says **the repository refused us** (`no_credential`) | no deploy key or token works for it | add the deploy key it shows you, or save a token |
| the agent says **the repository could not be reached** (`network`) | the fetch failed | try again; it also says when the last good look was |

The private tier is never shared. The company workspace is written only by the admin account. A connection label is at most 80 characters.

## How it works

### Flow

1. **Front page.** The terminal reads, in parallel: the workspace record, its tree, its last change, its
   remote status, and for a shared workspace its members and meetings. All go through the gateway to
   agent-api.
2. **Invite.** The agent calls its `workspace_invite` tool, which reaches
   `POST /api/workspace/invite`. When the workspace is one of your own private ones, agent-api first
   makes it shared and records you as its owner (the same step as `share-enable`); your personal
   workspace is never shared this way. A missing role is `contributor`. agent-api checks that you own the workspace, mints an invite
   restricted to that address and valid once, and builds the link from `VEXA_UI_URL`. For an address
   with no account, it publishes a `workspace.invited` event, and the flows service sends the email.
3. **Join.** `/join` previews the invite (`GET /api/join/preview`, which grants nothing), you sign in,
   and the terminal accepts it (`POST /api/workspace/invites/accept`). agent-api checks the
   gateway-verified email against the invite and adds you to the roster.
4. **Join links.** `workspace_invite_link_create` reaches `POST /api/workspace/invite-link`. agent-api
   checks you own the workspace (making your own private one shared first, as for an invite) and
   mints an *open* invite in the same store: a random 32-byte token, of which only its SHA-256 is
   kept, with `max_uses` and an expiry. `/join/<token>` redirects to `/join?i=<token>`, so it is
   previewed and accepted exactly like an invite. Each new member counts one use; on each, agent-api
   records the `workspace_joined_via_link` notification for whoever made the link and publishes
   `workspace.joined_via_link` to flows, where the company's `workspace_joined` mail kind can pick it
   up. Chats use the same tokens (`/api/join-links`), once the chat domain declares who may link one.
5. **Links.** `/w/<id>` is resolved by `GET /api/workspaces/{id}` (or `by-slug`), which always answers
   `readable`, `not-yours` or `gone`. `not-yours` names nothing: the chip is greyed and untitled, and
   the chat header never shows that workspace. The header says "Writes to X" only when the server
   says you can write X; otherwise it shows Personal. A group can be switched on in your active set
   only while you are a member of it.
   What the browser keeps (an open workspace, a pending meeting, layout) is stored per signed-in
   account, so a second account on the same browser starts from its own state.
   **Default for new chats.** "Make default for new chats" calls `PUT /api/chat/default-target`, which
   accepts only a workspace you can write (the same check as a chat's own target, `POST
   /api/chat/target`) and stores it per person. Each new chat reads `GET /api/chat/default-target`,
   which runs that check again and answers `null` (Personal, nothing named) when it fails. The
   terminal then mounts the workspace in the new chat and sets its target, through the same two
   calls a `focus` from the agent uses. No existing chat reads it.
5. **Attach.** `POST /api/workspace/import` starts an import and returns an operation id; the terminal
   polls `GET /api/workspace/import/{op}/status`. Deploy keys are `GET`/`POST
   /api/workspace/{slug}/deploy-key`; the saved token is `GET`/`POST /api/workspace/git-token` (only a
   masked value comes back).
   - The address is checked first, before any git process exists: the terminal's `repoRef.ts` and
     agent-api's `control_plane/repo_ref.py` accept the same shapes, nested GitLab paths included, and
     refuse the same ones; agent-api also refuses a host only it could reach.
   - The credential is resolved in `control_plane/workspace_credentials.py`: the workspace's deploy key
     for an `ssh` address, else a token typed for this call, else the person's saved token for the git
     provider the address is on (one per provider, in the credential broker).
   - A token is written into the clone, pull or push URL in exactly one place,
     `shared/token_destination.py`, and only for an `https` address. The shape of that userinfo comes
     from `shared/git_provider.py`: the token as typed when it names an account (`oauth2:…`,
     `<user>:…`), else by the configured provider (`oauth2:` for GitLab), else GitHub's bare form. After a clone the
     stored `origin` is reset to the token-free address.
6. **Publish.** `POST /api/workspace/publish` decides where a new repository would be created before it
   reads any credential (`resolve_provider` in `control_plane/workspace_publish.py`): a configured git
   provider, by name (`shared/git_provider.py`); anything else answers `422`. On GitLab it resolves the group with
   `GET /api/v4/namespaces/<group path>` and creates the project with `POST /api/v4/projects`, sending
   the token only in the `PRIVATE-TOKEN` header and refusing redirects, then pushes over the same
   token-scrubbed remote as every other push.
7. **Sync.** The agent's `workspace_status` tool reaches `POST /api/workspace/status`. agent-api
   resolves the workspace as it does for a pull (your own, or a shared one you are a member of), picks
   the same credential a pull would (the workspace's deploy key, or your saved token over `https`),
   and fetches the branch into its remote-tracking ref only (`refs/remotes/<home>/<branch>`). The
   working tree, the index and the local branch are not touched. It answers with the branch, upstream,
   ahead and behind counts, up to 50 commits on each side (sha, subject, number of files), a summary of
   uncommitted and untracked paths, and `fetched_at` / `previous_fetch_at`. `workspace_pull` and
   `workspace_push` stay separate and are the only verbs that change the branch or the repository.
8. **Connections.** The terminal's `/api/connections/*` routes call the credential broker as the
   signed-in person. Agents use their own `connection_request` route, which can request a setup but
   never read a credential.
9. **The admin account.** Identity decides it and nothing else does:
   - **Designation.** admin-api reads `VEXA_ADMIN_ACCOUNT`. When a named address signs in for the first
     time, `POST /admin/users` creates its row with `users.data.account = "admin"` and a bot cap of 0,
     in the same transaction. No other code path sets that field, and an existing row is never
     changed, so an everyday account cannot become the admin account. An account is the admin
     account only while it has the field **and** the address is still named.
   - **The answer.** admin-api reports `admin_account` on `POST /internal/validate` (identity.v1; for
     a worker, `person_admin_account`) and on `GET /internal/users/{id}/is-admin`. The gateway reports
     it on `/auth/me`; the terminal's `/api/auth/me` passes it to the page. agent-api reads it in one
     function, `global_layer.is_admin_account`, which answers *no* when identity is unreachable or
     the field is missing.
   - **Writing `_global`.** The dispatch mounts `_global` read-write only for the admin account, and
     the file, page, reset, acceptance and chat-target routes ask the same function. The MCP spends
     flows' operator key on `flows_submit` and `flow_lifecycle` only when `/auth/me` says
     `admin_account: true`.
   - **Having nothing else.** For the admin account the dispatch mounts `_global` and `_system` only
     and makes `_global` the working directory; it seeds no desk. agent-api's workspace door
     (`_read_target`) resolves no other workspace for it, an app-level dependency
     (`admin_account_scope.REFUSED`) refuses the routes that create, import, activate or join a
     workspace and every meeting route, and `GET /api/workspace/active` answers `_global` with
     `admin_account: true`. Its bot cap of 0 makes meeting-api refuse any bot it asks for.

### Who writes what

| Data | Single writer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Workspace files and roster (`policy/members.json`) | agent-api | the workspace's git repository |
| Invites (hashes only) | agent-api | `.invites/` beside the workspaces, outside every workspace |
| Workspace ids and names | agent-api | `.vexa/workspace.json` in each workspace, plus a registry that can be rebuilt from it |
| Membership index, users by email | admin-api | the identity database |
| Which account is the admin account (`users.data.account`) | admin-api, when it creates the row | the identity database |
| The company workspace `_global` | the admin account's chats and routes, through agent-api | `_global`'s git repository; every change is a commit by its author |
| Import status | agent-api | outside every workspace |
| Your default for new chats | agent-api | its session index, per person; re-checked on every read |
| Git tokens and deploy keys | the credential broker (agent-api's `git` role) | its encrypted store |
| Connections, consent state, audit | the credential broker | its encrypted store |

| agent tools `workspace_status`, `workspace_pull`, `workspace_push` | agent-api `/api/workspace/status`, `/pull`, `/push` |

### Configuration

`VEXA_UI_URL` on agent-api (no invite is made without it). `VEXA_GIT_PROVIDERS` on agent-api (Helm
`agentApi.gitProviders`) registers self-hosted git hosts; it is validated when agent-api starts.
`VEXA_ADMIN_ACCOUNT` on admin-api names the admin account (compose `.env`, Helm
`adminApi.adminAccount`, Lite `-e`); see [Configuration](/configuration). agent-api's old
`VEXA_GLOBAL_ADMIN_SUBJECTS` is retired and ignored. The terminal's connections routes read
`VEXA_CONNECTIONS_BROKER_URL`, `VEXA_CONNECTIONS_HUMAN_KEY_FILE` and `VEXA_CONNECTIONS_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`;
the broker itself is set up as in [Connections](/connections).

## Why it complies

### Architecture

Workspace names: `control_plane/workspace_names.py` lists every workspace a person has (personal,
private, shared) under the names the Files pane uses, returned as `all` by `GET /api/workspace/shared`
(the `workspaces` tool). A middleware in agent-api resolves a name to its slug for every workspace

### Security

**Who can do what.** Owners add, remove and change members and manage the repository. Contributors
write. Readers read. Only the owner or an administrator renames a workspace. The private tier cannot be
shared, and only the admin account writes the company workspace. The admin account has no desk, no
shared workspaces and no meetings.

**A default for new chats is a pointer, never a grant.** It is stored only after the same writable
check a chat's target gets, and checked again on every read, so being removed, a deleted workspace or
a role lowered to reader turns it back into Personal without naming the workspace
(`core/agent/tests/test_chat_default_target.py`). The company workspace is never an everyday default.

**A join link is a capability with limits.** The token is unguessable (32 random bytes) and stored
only as its hash, so a copy of the store opens nothing. Only the workspace's owner mints one; it
grants at most `max_uses` new members, the role it was minted with (never owner), and nothing after
it expires or is revoked. Redeeming counts uses under a per-workspace lock, so concurrent opens cannot
exceed the limit. Every join notifies the link's maker, so a link that travelled further than meant
is visible at once and can be revoked. Deny tests: `core/agent/tests/test_join_links.py` ·
`test_only_the_owner_mints_a_link`, `test_revoked_and_expired_links_refuse`,
`test_three_uses_then_a_calm_refusal_and_a_member_reopening_spends_nothing`,
`test_a_link_is_a_join_url_with_an_unguessable_token_stored_only_as_its_hash`; a delegated run is
held to its ceiling on all three link routes by `core/agent/tests/test_delegation_ceiling_verbs.py` ·
`test_a_workspace_outside_the_ceiling_is_refused_and_nothing_changes`.

**Protections.**

- Invite tokens are random, shown once, and stored only as a hash. They expire, can be withdrawn, and
  are used once under a lock, so two people cannot redeem the same invite.
- A restricted invite is checked against the email the gateway verified, not one the browser sends.
- The invite preview grants nothing and does not say whether a workspace exists.
- A repository address is a whitelist: plain path segments only, never empty, `.`, `..` or `-`, at
  most 22 deep, exactly two on `github.com`; a host only agent-api could reach is refused, nested path
  or not.
- A saved token goes only to `https://github.com/`. A token for another host is typed for one call and
  travels only over `https`. Publish sends a token to an API only on `github.com` or a host the operator
  registered as GitLab, and never follows a redirect with it.
- Deploy keys are generated server-side; the private half is kept in the credential broker and written
  to a permission-`0600` file only for the length of one git operation.
- **Looking at the repository changes nothing.** `workspace_status` fetches into the remote-tracking ref
  only; the working tree, the index and the local branch stay as they were, which a test checks
  byte for byte. Because it changes nothing a person sees, it is allowed in the background regime
  (no person watching); a pull and a push still need a person. Any member may look, a viewer
  included; a pull needs contributor because it rewrites the tree. The agent never runs git: its
  sandbox cannot write `.git`, so these three verbs are the only way it syncs.
- Connection routes accept only the deployment's own origin and a closed set of actions. The terminal
  holds the person's broker key; agent workers hold none.
- Consent pop-ups follow only Google's authorization address or the address the setup declared, and
  report a status, never a credential, back to the page.
- The admin account is decided by admin-api, which every other service asks. Both conditions must
  hold: the row was created as the admin account, and the address is still named. With nothing
  configured, with identity unreachable, or with an older identity that does not send the field,
  nobody writes `_global`.
- An everyday account is never promoted: the admin field is written only when admin-api creates the
  row.
- The admin account is confined three ways: its mounts, the workspace door, and a list of refused
  routes that `admin_account_scope.assert_served` checks at boot, so a renamed route cannot leave a
  gap unnoticed.
