A **chat** has exactly one person: you and your agent. A **room** is shared: it lives in a workspace,
holds no one's personal data, and Vexa in it runs as the workspace, never as any one person. Two people
talking is a room too, a **room of two**.

## What you can do

### See who is in a room

The people beside the room's name open the **members panel**:

- **In #room**: each member, how they got there ("added by Dmitry"), and whether they have opened
  it yet. Someone who never opened it shows a faded face.
- **Invited, not on Vexa yet**: people invited from this room, and when the invite runs out.
- **In the workspace, not here** (closed rooms): each workspace member who is not in the room, with **Add**.

An **open room** includes every member of its workspace. Leaving the workspace takes you out of its
open rooms. A **closed room** has only the people added to it.

### Tag someone with @

Tagging someone posts your message. **A mention never changes who has access by itself**: if it
would, only you get a card, and nothing happens until you answer it.

| You tag | What happens |
|---|---|
| A room member | They are mentioned and notified. No card. |
| A workspace member who is not in the room | A card only you see: **Add and tell them** · **Yes, and always for this workspace** · **Just mention**. |
| Someone outside the workspace (a contact, or someone from another of your workspaces) | A card: **Invite and tell them** · **Just mention**. The invite is for the workspace and this room, bound to their address, as a contributor (a reader if you are one). They land in the room after they accept. |
| An address nobody knows | The same invite card. Your workspace's policy decides between a guest seat and an invite; guest seats are not available yet, so it is an invite. |

**"Always"** adds one readable line to the workspace's policy: *"In Harbor AI Meetup, when Dmitry
@mentions a workspace member who isn't in the room, add them and tell them."* If you own the workspace,
the line applies at once. Otherwise it goes to the owner to approve in the app. Once it is in force,
tagging a workspace member adds them without a card, and a one-line receipt offers **Undo**.

"Always" is never offered for bringing someone new into a workspace. That is decided person by person.

### Invite without writing a message

**Invite** in the room's header opens the same picker: type a name or an address. Each pick produces
the same card as a tag.

### Message a person

**Message** on a person opens your room of two with them, creating it the first time. The same two
people always land in the same room. Vexa is there with no access to either person's own data;
personal questions get "ask me in your chat". A room of two holds exactly two people.

### Ask Vexa to call someone

In your own chat, say **"Call Maya"** (or `@Maya`, or their address). Vexa finds Maya among the people
you share a workspace with, opens your room of two with her, starts a Vexa call there and rings her in
the app. The room opens and you are in the call at once, shown as calling Maya with **Cancel** until
she joins; the ring lasts 30 seconds. If two people match,
Vexa asks which one; if nobody you share a workspace with matches, it asks who you mean. A Vexa call
is Vexa's own call, not a Meet or Zoom link. Sending Vexa into an existing Meet, Teams or Zoom link
is a different thing.

`@` works in your private chats too: it offers the people you share a workspace with. A pick is
**Tag** (the agent knows who you mean; the chat stays private) or **Call Maya** (rings her in your room
of two).

### Start a meeting

**Start a meeting** on the Meetings page offers three choices:

- **Call a person**: the people you share a workspace with. **Call** rings them in your room of two.
- **Start in a room**: your rooms. A room with a live call says **Rejoin**.
- **Schedule for later**: plan the call in a room. Nothing reaches a calendar until you add it
  yourself. Without rooms it shows as coming.

A Vexa call is the default; Google Meet, Teams and Zoom are under **Other options** (paste their link
in **Send Vexa to a meeting**).

**One live call per place.** Starting a call where one of yours is already live asks first: *"Call with
Maya is still running. Rejoin it?"* **Rejoin** joins it; **Start a new one** starts another. For a
call with a person, a new one ends the old call only if nobody else is in it.

**Names.** A call is named after who or where: "Call with Maya" in a room of two (each of you reads the
other's name), "#pilot call" in a room. Once the call has a topic, Vexa renames it ("#pilot · ADFS test
window") and the row says **renamed**. A name you typed is never replaced.

**Live and Recent.** A live row shows who is in now, with their faces, and how long the call has run.
When the last person leaves, the call ends on its own and moves to **Recent**: "ended when everyone left
· 12 min".

### Start a call and ring people

**Start call** in a room rings the room's members, and only them:

- In a **closed room**, everyone in it is rung.
- In an **open room**, members who opened the room in the last 30 days are rung. The others see the
  live call on the workspace page and can join, but they are not rung.
- Someone added while the call is live is rung at once.
- If nobody else is in the room, the call says so and how to ring someone.

Joining a call no longer gives you its transcript automatically. The call's owner shares it with each
person explicitly. A room's call notes land in the room, which its members already read.

### Switch Vexa or transcription off

The room's **⋯** menu has two switches:

- **Vexa in this room**: off means no agent replies, no listening, and no cards proposed from the room.
- **Transcribe calls**: off means calls here have audio only, with no transcript or notes.

| Room | Turning a switch off | Turning it back on |
|---|---|---|
| Room of two | Either person | Needs both: your request becomes a card for the other person (**Turn on** · **Keep off**) |
| Bigger room | The room's owner or a workspace owner | The same |

A workspace's policy can lock either switch; the menu then says which rule locks it. When either
switch is off, a small indicator sits beside the room's name.

On a phone (below 520 px), the header shows the name, the people and **⋯**. Start call, Invite and
both switches move into **⋯**.

### Shared chats became rooms

Chats with more than one person were retired in 0.13.4. Existing ones moved once, and everyone in each
got one notice:

- **Two people**: the chat became their room of two. If they already had one, it became a second room beside it.
- **Three or more**: a closed room in the workspace the chat wrote to, with the same people.
- **A chat that wrote to no workspace**: not moved; its owner is asked to choose a workspace.

History and links are unchanged.

## Limits and errors

| You see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "A room of two has exactly two people" | You tagged or invited a third person in a room of two | Start a room in a workspace |
| "In this room its owners change the switches" | You are a member of a bigger room | Ask an owner |
| "… is locked … by policy" | The workspace's policy locks that switch | Ask the workspace's owner |
| "Calls in this room are not transcribed" | Transcribe calls is off | Start the call without a transcript, or ask to turn it back on |
| No card after a tag | The person is already in the room | Nothing: they are notified |
| "Which … do you mean?" after "Call …" | More than one person you share a workspace with matches | Answer with the one you mean |
| Vexa asks who you mean after "Call …" | Nobody you share a workspace with matches | Name them as People shows them, or give their address to invite them |
| "Confirm …" before a call rings | This chat read your private data, and the ring is a notice to someone else | Reply with the confirm code to ring them once |
| "They were rung too many times just now" | You rang that person 3 times (or anyone 30 times) in the last 10 minutes | Wait the seconds it names, then call again |

## How it works

### Who is in a room (D36)

agent-api decides who is in a room. The session store's participant rows are kept as a
**projection** only (read marks, your sidebar, who opened the room recently); they never decide access.
For every session that has a room row, the room-membership resolver (`control_plane/room_membership.py`)
answers each access check:

- **Open room**: derived from the workspace's roster in the identity registry. A workspace member is a
  room member (every member takes part; a reader's turn still runs read-only). The room's maker stays
  its owner while they are in the workspace.
- **Closed room**: explicit `room:<session id>` rows in the identity registry's `memberships` table,
  written through admin-api's `/internal/registry/rooms/{id}/members` doors. Without identity, the file
  backend keeps `<root>/.rooms/<id>.json`, outside every workspace mount.
- **Migration**: a closed room with no registry rows yet adopts, once, the rows the file backend kept
  for it (`.rooms/<id>.json`), else its participant rows, while `VEXA_POLICY_LEGACY_READ` is on.
- **Fail closed**: a roster or row set that cannot be read means no membership.

Closing an open room writes rows for the people who were following it. Opening a closed room drops its rows.

### Tagging (D21)

The composer posts the message, then asks `POST /api/rooms/{session}/tag` for each person the message
mentions. The server answers the case and the exact card. The card's buttons call
`POST /api/rooms/{session}/members`, which every delegated caller is refused.

- **A workspace member** is added: registry row first for a closed room, then the projection, a
  `room_added` notice, a system line, and a ring if a call is live.
- **"Always"** builds the workspace's new rule list and records it as a `policy` proposal (the S1
  proposal mechanism, I15). The owner's own click from a browser session applies it with
  `Approved-by` and `On-behalf-of` trailers. The rule is `{"action": "room.mention_adds_member",
  "principals": [<tagger>]}`, evaluated by the one policy evaluator.
- **Everyone else** gets a workspace invite (`membership_acts.invite`, restricted to the address,
  role capped at the tagger's), carrying the room as pending membership. Accepting the invite adds
  them to that room.
- **Guest seats** are refused by the platform floor (`floor.no-guest-seats`) until audience-limited
  mounts exist. Decision 9's seeded default, invite, is what happens.

### Rooms of two (FD4)

`POST /api/rooms/direct` finds or creates the pair's hidden workspace. Its id is
`pair-<sha256 of the two account ids, ordered>`, so a race cannot make two. Both people are owners;
the room's `created_by` (who pays) is the person who opened it. The workspace records `kind: pair`
in the registry. A pair workspace is never in the membership index, a workspace list, a mount or the
people directory. Every membership grant refuses a third person.

### Calling a person

`POST /api/rooms/call` (the agent's `call_person`) composes the pieces above. It matches `person` in
the caller's directory only (account id, then a whole name or address, then a name's leading words);
several matches answer 409 with names and shared workspaces, never an address. It finds or creates the
room of two (`pair_rooms.open_room`), starts the call on meeting-api with the caller's own signed
identity forwarded unchanged (so the caller's regime, scopes and bot limit apply, and the meeting is
theirs), binds it to the room as Start call does, and rings the other person. With Transcribe calls off
the call starts untranscribed. `room` instead of `person` calls an existing room. A call already live
in the room is reused and the person is rung again. `fresh: true` (the person's own **Start a new one**,
ignored from an agent) ends the caller's own live call there when nobody else is in it, then starts a
new one; otherwise 409 `still_in_call`.

**Names.** Where a call starts in a room (`call_person`, or `POST /api/sessions/{id}/call` from Start
call), agent-api writes its name on the meeting row through meeting-api's owner-scoped annotate:
`title` "Call with <first name>" or "#<room> call", plus metadata `call_named`, `call_kind` and, for a
room of two, `call_with` (both people, so each reads the other's name). A private chat's call is not
named here. When the caller's chat list is read, their live calls whose title is still exactly
`call_named` are renamed once by topic: the live transcript (`tc:meeting:{id}`) is given to the cheap
model (`model_home`, within the spend cap) for at most five words, and the title becomes
"#pilot · <topic>" with `call_renamed_from` recorded. No model call is spent before the transcript has
something to name, and at most three per call.

**Who is in now.** `GET /api/sessions/{id}/call` answers `call.in_now`: the room's participants by
name, without the hidden listener. The call ends when the last person leaves: the listener leaves after
its grace (`emptyGraceSec`, 30 s by default) and meeting-api records `completion_reason: left_alone`,
which the Meetings page shows as "ended when everyone left".

The answer carries `ui_action: "join_call"`. The harness turns it into a `call` event (ids and the other
person's name only), and the terminal joins as the caller: only in the view whose own message placed
the call, only on the live stream, and only after `GET /api/sessions/{id}/call` shows the call is still
that one and its `owner` is the reader (`you`). The caller's join is what keeps the call past
meeting-api's first-join window (`CALL_FIRST_JOIN_TIMEOUT_SEC`, 30 s).

### Switches

The switches are columns on the room row (`agent_on`, `transcribe`, plus waiting turn-on asks), and
`control_plane/room_switches.py` decides who may change them:

- Who may switch is a default policy line: either person in a room of two, owners in a bigger room.
- A workspace DENY rule on `room.switch.<vexa|transcribe>.<on|off>` locks a switch.

They are enforced where each effect starts:

- with Vexa off, `session_turn.plan_turn` starts no turn, the `room.signal` door refuses with
  `room_vexa_off`, and the listen brief wakes on nothing;
- with transcription off, binding a transcribed call answers 409.

### Ringing

`control_plane/room_ring.py` sends each rung member a live `ring` frame on their own channel and a
`call_started` notice that expires after 30 minutes. Who counts is `room_membership.ringable`. The
tuning line for open rooms is `VEXA_ROOM_RING_DERIVED_DAYS` (default 30).

Every ring passes a limit, in `ring` itself: one caller rings one person at most
`VEXA_ROOM_RING_PER_PERSON` times (default 3) and anyone at most `VEXA_ROOM_RING_PER_CALLER` times
(default 30) in any 10 minutes. A person over it is not rung and is not in `rang`. `call_person` checks
first and answers 429 `ring_rate_limited` with `Retry-After`, starting nothing. The count is kept in
agent-api's process (one replica); a restart forgives at most one window.

### Configuration

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `VEXA_ROOM_RING_DERIVED_DAYS` | `30` | An open room rings members who opened it within this many days |
| `VEXA_ROOM_RING_PER_PERSON` | `3` | Rings from one caller to one person in any 10 minutes |
| `VEXA_ROOM_RING_PER_CALLER` | `30` | Rings from one caller to anyone in any 10 minutes |
| `VEXA_POLICY_LEGACY_READ` | `1` | While on, a closed room adopts its participant rows once (set to `0` after every instance has started on 0.13.4) |

The one-time shared-chat migration is `POST /internal/rooms/migrate-shared-chats?dry_run=true` on the
internal tier: run it as a dry run, read the report, then run it with `dry_run=false`.

## Why it complies

### Architecture

| Rule | How | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P20** complete mediation | Every room access check asks one resolver; the participant projection grants nothing | deny tests below |
| **P23** one writer | Closed-room rows have one writer (identity, through agent-api's registry port); the projection is agent-api's | `gate:dataflow` |
| **ADR-0042 / FD3** fail closed | An unreadable roster, room row set or rule set refuses | `test_an_unreadable_room_registry_refuses` |
| **R9** a mention never changes access | A tag only classifies; every grant is a separate person's click | `test_case2_…`, deny 29 |
| **R12** behaviours are policy lines | Guest vs invite is a floor line; switches are default lines a workspace can lock; ringing derived members is a tuning line | `test_in_a_bigger_room_owners_switch_and_policy_can_lock` |
| **P18** fail loud | Locks answer 423 naming the rule; refusals carry the server's own sentence | tests |

### Security

- **No directory oracle (M1).** The @ picker leaves out undiscoverable members and shows readers names
  only (deny test 16). An invite by address answers the same whether or not the address has an
  account. The unauthenticated invite preview shows the workspace's name and terms (and, for an
  invite bound to an address, that address, so the page can prefill it); never its purpose or the
  sender's address. The signed-in confirm card shows the sender.
- **Only a person mints an invite (H3).** Every door that mints an invite or a join link
  (`/api/workspace/invites`, `/api/workspace/invite`, `/api/workspace/invite-link`, `/api/join-links`,
  and the tag card's invite) takes only a person's session: no delegation token of any regime, a
  person's own watched chat agent included, and no API key without the browser scope. An agent
  proposes; the person's click on the card is the act. When S5's signed cards land, the agent's
  proposal becomes an invitation card the person signs with the session credential (D13), and the
  platform mints only after that signature.
- **No inherited transcript (M8).** Joining a call does not redeem its transcript share. The owner
  shares it per person (`POST /api/sessions/{s}/call/share`).
- **Agents never add people (deny test 29).** An agent-composed mention (`origin: agent`, or any
  delegated caller) is never offered "always", never acts on an existing line (`floor.agent-mentions-never-add`),
  and the add route refuses every delegated caller.
- **Only a person in the chat calls someone.** `POST /api/rooms/call` refuses an unwatched agent
  (`person: true`), a turn other people read, and a room's workspace principal
  (`floor.ws-principal-never-calls-a-person`). A workspace DENY rule on `call.person` stops calls in its
  rooms. The ring is a notice to someone else, so a chat that read private data holds it until the
  person confirms. The request carries no text: nothing the agent writes reaches the other person.
- **Vexa off means nothing runs (deny test 25).** No turn, signal or card proposal from a room with Vexa off.
- **Pair workspaces stay private to the pair.** They are hidden from every enumeration, and a third
  member is refused at the one grant function.
