title: "Your photo"
description: "The picture people who work with you see beside your name: where it comes from, how to change it, and who can see it."

Wherever you appear in Vexa (avatar stacks, hover cards, the People strip, @-mentions, chat
bubbles, a call with your camera off), the picture shown is, in this order:

1. **A photo you uploaded.** Use **Settings → Your photo → Change photo**, or **Change photo** on
   your own avatar. Use a PNG, JPEG or WebP; a large picture is made smaller in your browser
   first.
2. **Your sign-in provider's photo**, if you sign in with Google, Microsoft or your company's
   identity provider and it has one.
3. **Your Gravatar**, only if you have one.
4. **Your initials** on a colour that stays the same every time.

**Remove photo** removes your upload, and the next one in the list shows. Only you can change your
photo. Your agent can suggest a picture, but it cannot set one.

People who are not Vexa users, such as contacts in your CRM or guests in your meetings, are never
shown with a face. They get their initials and their company's logo as a small badge. A face
appears only when they join Vexa and bring their own.

## How it works

- **Identity keeps the photo.** admin-api is the only service that writes it. For each source
  (upload, provider, Gravatar) it keeps one image in the install's object store, under
  `person-photos/<account>/` in the same bucket recordings use. Its own `person_photos` table holds
  only the object's key, type and checksum, so the database every sign-in check reads carries no
  images. It accepts only a PNG, JPEG or WebP of at most 200 KB and at most 4096 pixels on each
  side. It checks the bytes themselves, so the claimed type and the file name don't count. SVG and
  GIF are refused.
- **Only the pixels are kept.** Every stored image (upload, provider photo or Gravatar) is decoded
  and written again from its pixels alone. EXIF (including GPS location), XMP, colour profiles,
  PNG text and comments are all dropped. A photo is turned upright first, and at most 1024
  pixels on a side is kept.
- **Identity fetches the provider photo itself, when you sign in.** For Google and OIDC, the
  sign-in hands admin-api its own ID token. admin-api verifies the token itself against the
  provider's published keys. It checks the issuer, that the token was issued to this Vexa
  deployment, that it has not expired, and that it belongs to the account linked to yours. Only
  then does it read the `picture` address from the token (for Google, only an `https` address on
  Google's own picture host) and fetch it through the same outbound guard that webhooks and calendar feeds use. That guard refuses private and internal addresses,
  including after redirects, and anything that is not a small image. For Microsoft, the sign-in
  passes on its own access token for that one call (no extra permission). admin-api asks Microsoft
  Graph whose token it is, and takes the photo only if it belongs to the Microsoft account linked
  to yours. No token is kept. No service can name a picture address or hand over picture bytes
  for anyone. At each later sign-in, the photo is fetched again if its address changed or a week
  has passed, and removed if your provider no longer has one. Sign-in never waits for any of this.
- **Gravatar is checked only for your own address, and only when you have no better photo.** The
  request uses `d=404`, so having no Gravatar means no picture rather than a generated one. The
  answer is kept for a week, including a miss. An operator can switch this off with
  `VEXA_PERSON_GRAVATAR=false` on admin-api.
- **Shown only through Vexa.** Browsers fetch `/api/people/{account}/photo` from agent-api with
  their own sign-in. On every request, agent-api checks that the viewer is you, or shares a
  workspace or a chat with you (a call happens in a chat). Only then does it ask identity for the
  bytes. Everyone else gets the same "not found" as a person without a photo. A photo is cached
  for 30 seconds in agent-api and for 30 seconds, privately, in the viewer's browser. A removed or
  changed photo therefore disappears for everyone within a minute, and for you at once.
- **Without an object store** (an install with no S3 configured), no photos are kept and everyone
  shows initials.
- **Operators.** admin-api verifies ID tokens with the values the app signs people in with:
  `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` for Google, `VEXA_OIDC_ISSUER` and `VEXA_OIDC_CLIENT_ID` (and
  `VEXA_OIDC_CA_FILE` for a provider under an internal CA) for OIDC. Compose and the Helm chart set
  them. A provider whose client id admin-api does not have gives no photo. Upgrade and rollback are in
  the changelog.

## Why it complies

- **You decide your face.** Your upload overrides everything else. Only you can set it or remove
  it, with your own sign-in. A worker acting for you is refused, even while you are in the chat.
- **Visible only to people who work with you.** You share a workspace or a chat with each person who
  can see your photo. Simply being on the same deployment is not enough.
- **No face is looked up on the web.** Vexa never searches for a person by name or address. The
  only outbound requests are made at your own sign-in: one to the provider you signed in with, and
  one to Gravatar for your own address (if that is switched on). Nothing is fetched for anyone else.
- **No hidden data in a photo.** What colleagues receive has no location, camera or editing
  metadata, whatever the original file carried.
- **Deleted with you.** Deleting an account deletes its photo records and its objects in the
  store.
