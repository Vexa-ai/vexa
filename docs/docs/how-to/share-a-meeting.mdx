---
title: "Share a meeting"
description: "Share a meeting while it runs and after it ends: invite people by email, add its workspace, see who has access, and take access back."
---

You can share a meeting while it is live or after it has ended. The people you share it with see the
**live transcript and the bot's status** during the meeting. Afterwards they see the **transcript,
the notes and the recording**; the recording only if you allow it. Everyone you share with signs in
first. There is no public, anonymous link.

## Share from the terminal

Open the meeting and press **Share** in its header, either in the meeting view or in the meeting's
page header. You can use the dialog while the meeting is running.

1. **Invite people.** Type one or more email addresses and press **Invite**. Each person is emailed
   their own link, and that link works only for their address: if it is forwarded, it admits nobody
   else. The dialog says who was emailed. You can also copy each link, and if an email could not be
   sent, the dialog says so and the copied link is the way to reach them.
2. **Add the workspace (optional).** If the meeting belongs to a workspace, choose **can view** or
   **can edit** to bundle a workspace invite into the copied link. The email carries the meeting
   only, so send the copied link to anyone you also want in the workspace; the dialog says so. The
   invitee sees what the workspace is before they join it.
3. **Create a link (optional).** **Create a link** makes a link that anyone can open after signing in.
   It lasts seven days. **Turn off** withdraws it, and everyone who came in through it loses access —
   unless you also invited them by address, in which case that invite still admits them. A reader
   with no record of how they came in (one who joined before this release and has not been named
   yet) is removed too; invite them again if they should stay.
4. **The recording.** Tick **People you share with can play and download the recording** to give them
   the recording too. It is off by default, and until you tick it they do not see that a recording
   exists — no ids, file sizes or storage keys.

**People with access** lists everyone who can read the meeting, with their role: readers (*can view*),
invites nobody has opened yet, and the members of the meeting's workspace. **Remove** takes a person's
access away at once on every page they load, and ends a live transcript they have open within about
17 seconds. Removing someone from the workspace
is the workspace owner's action, the same as on the workspace's own page.

A meeting you share shows a quiet **Shared with N** in your lists. A meeting shared with you shows
**Shared with you**. You can read it, but you cannot stop its bot, delete it or change who has access.

## Open a meeting someone shared with you

Open the link and sign in with the address it was sent to. The meeting appears in your meetings and
opens directly on the meeting's own page — its transcript, its status and **Shared with you** — with
your desk one tab away. When the link cannot be used, the terminal says why instead of opening an empty page:

| You see | Why |
|---|---|
| *This meeting was shared with a different email address.* | You signed in as someone else. Sign in with the invited address. |
| *The owner has withdrawn this share, or removed your access.* | The invite or link was turned off, or you were removed. |
| *This share link has expired.* | Invites last 30 days and links 7. Ask the owner for a new one. |
| *Access removed* (in the meeting) | You were removed while watching. The live transcript stops and does not reconnect. |

## Limits

- The invite email goes through the deployment's mail service (flows). A deployment without one
  sends nothing, and the dialog says the email could not be sent; the copied link still works.
- **Putting a meeting in a workspace takes edit access there.** A member who can only view a
  workspace cannot bind a meeting to it — the dialog, a planned meeting and a bot sent into the
  workspace are all refused with *you need edit access*, and an agent worker running with nobody in
  the loop cannot bind a meeting at all. A new occurrence of a calendar series joins
  the series' workspace only while you can still edit it; otherwise it is created private.
- **One email per invite, 30 per hour.** Each invite email goes to exactly one address, and an owner
  can have at most 30 sent in an hour across all their meetings; past that the dialog says so and the
  copied link still works. An invite address must be a single plain address.
- An **invite** lasts 30 days and a **link** 7 days. Removing a person blocks every link minted before the
  removal; inviting them again works.
- **Role on the meeting is view-only.** Edit rights exist on the workspace (*can edit*), not on the
  meeting itself.
- A removed reader's open live view ends within **about 17 seconds** on the terminal's stream (a
  10-second re-check, a read that blocks for at most 5 seconds, and one retry) and within **about 15
  seconds** on `/ws`.
- A workspace member already sees the meeting through the workspace. The dialog shows them, but
  only the workspace owner removes them.

## How it works

1. **The owner mints an invite.** The terminal calls `POST /meetings/{id}/share`
   (`mode: "restricted"`, `allowed_emails: [one address]`), once per address. meeting-api stores only
   the token's hash, in the meeting row (`data.share_grants`), and returns the token once. If the
   workspace is included, the terminal also mints an agent-api workspace invite restricted to the same
   address (`POST /agent/workspace/invites`). The link is `/?tshare=<token>[&invite=<token>]`.
2. **The recipient signs in and redeems.** The terminal's arrival gate posts the token to
   `POST /transcripts/share/accept`. The gateway attaches the caller's verified identity and email.
   meeting-api checks the hash, the expiry, the revocation, the email allow-list and any removal, then
   adds the caller to `data.transcript_viewers` and records who they are in `data.share_viewers` —
   with every grant they came in through (`grant_ids`), so turning one link off takes away exactly
   the access it gave.
3. **Every read asks the same question.** meeting-api answers *may this caller read this meeting?* with
   one access union: the owner, a reader in `transcript_viewers`, or a member of the bound workspace.
   That union backs the list, the detail and the transcript. The `/ws` subscribe check applies the same
   three branches in its own query, keyed by (platform, native id); the live re-check then compares
   the meeting ROW each subscription streams, so a recurring code that reaches another row never
   keeps a removed reader on this one. agent-api's live
   stream and the meeting note ask meeting-api the same question as the caller. Recording reads add one
   more condition: the owner's `data.share_settings.recording`.
4. **The owner sees and edits access.** `GET /meetings/{id}/access` returns readers, pending invites,
   links, the workspace and the recording setting, without tokens or hashes. `DELETE …/viewers/{viewer_id}`,
   `DELETE …/share/{grant}` and `PATCH …/access` change it under the meeting row's lock.
   **Readers from before the roster.** Someone who redeemed a share before meeting-api kept
   `share_viewers` is known only by id. On the owner's first access view, meeting-api asks identity
   for their address over the internal tier (`GET /internal/users/{id}/email`, `X-Internal-Secret`),
   once per reader, and writes the roster entry a redeem writes today, including the invite they came
   in through where the record shows it. That invite then stops showing as pending, and removing them
   withdraws it. When they joined is not recorded, so it stays empty. If identity cannot answer, they
   stay listed by id and are still removable, and that reader is not asked about again for a day.
   At most 25 readers are looked up per view, concurrently.
5. **The invite email.** With `notify: true` — only for a grant restricted to exactly one address, and
   at most 30 per owner per hour — meeting-api hands one `meeting.shared` fact to flows
   (`meeting_api/events.py`, the existing publish edge, bounded and best-effort). The fact carries **no
   token**: the meeting, the address, the owner and the grant's id. flows' `meeting_share` mints that
   person's own restricted link at send time, as the owner, through meeting-api's share mint — naming
   the owner's grant (`requires_grant`), so meeting-api refuses the mint with `409` if that invite was
   withdrawn, or its person removed, after the owner pressed Invite; then no mail goes out — renders
   `behavior/mail/meeting-share.md` (the owner's title cut to one plain line of at most 200
   characters) and appends the link it composes from its own `VEXA_UI_URL` (`/?tshare=…`). No producer
   and no template writes a URL, and no working token is stored outside meeting-api's hashes. The mint
   answers `notified: {address: true|false}` so the dialog can say which emails were handed over.
6. **Live views are re-checked while open.** agent-api's `/api/meeting/stream` asks the access union
   again every 10 seconds, and also notices a transcript the owner deleted. If the answer is no twice,
   it ends with an `access-revoked` event. The gateway re-authorizes every open `/ws` subscription every
   15 seconds, by the row it streams, and stops a revoked one with `subscription_revoked`. It also re-resolves the key, so an ended workspace membership drops that
   workspace's status channel.

**Who writes what.** One writer per carrier:

| Data | Owner (sole writer) |
|---|---|
| `meeting.data.share_grants`, `transcript_viewers`, `share_viewers`, `share_removed`, `share_settings` | meeting-api (`collector/share_access.py`, under the row lock) |
| Workspace membership (`policy/members.json`) and workspace invites | agent-api |
| The writable-workspace set on an identity (`writable_workspaces`) | identity (admin-api `/internal/validate`), signed by the gateway |
| The `meeting.shared` fact and its mail | meeting-api publishes; flows mails (`meeting_share`) |
| The live transcript stream (`tc:meeting:{id}`) | meeting-api's collector. agent-api and the gateway only read it. |

**Contracts and routes.** The meetings route table `core/meetings/routes.v1.json` carries the four new
owner routes (`tx` scope). `ws.v1` gains the `subscription_revoked` error code and a golden. The
terminal's SSE event `access-revoked` is agent-api ↔ terminal. The API reference is in
[Meetings API › Share a meeting](/api/meetings#share-a-meeting).

**Configuration.** None. The re-check intervals (10 s on the stream, 15 s on `/ws`), the hourly email cap
(30) and the invite and link lifetimes (30 and 7 days) are constants in `routers/meetings.py`,
`gateway/multiplex.py`, `collector/app.py` and `meetingShareApi.ts`, and the server clamps any requested
lifetime to 30 days.

### Security

**Who can access what.** Only the owner can mint, list, revoke, remove or change the recording setting.
A non-owner gets the same `404` as for a meeting that doesn't exist. A reader can read the meeting,
its transcript and live feed, and its recording if the owner allowed it. A reader cannot delete,
stop the bot or see other readers. The roster (`transcript_viewers`, `share_viewers`, `share_removed`)
and the grants are stripped from every response to anyone but the owner, and the grants are stripped
from the owner's responses too.

identity's `GET /internal/users/{id}/email` answers only the internal tier and only `{id, email}`;
meeting-api calls it only on the owner's own access view, for readers already on that meeting.

**How each hop enforces it.** The gateway authenticates the key and signs the caller's identity and
email onto the hop. meeting-api trusts only that signature, or the internal secret for agent-api's
as-the-caller lookups, and decides access itself. agent-api never decides meeting access locally. The
gateway re-runs meeting-api's subscribe check for open sockets.

**What is refused.** Binding a meeting into a workspace the caller cannot write (a viewer, a
non-member, or an identity without the write claim — fail closed); a share email for an open link
or for a meeting the caller does not own; redeeming a restricted invite from another address; redeeming a revoked or expired
grant; redeeming any grant minted before the redeemer was removed; owner routes called by anyone else;
recording reads — and recording metadata — without the owner's permission; an invite entry that is not
exactly one address; a share email to a list, or past the owner's hourly cap; a send-time link for an invite that was
withdrawn, or whose person was removed, since it was made; a workspace bind by an unwatched worker;
and every read by a
person who was never invited.
