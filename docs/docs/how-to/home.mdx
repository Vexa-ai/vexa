A new chat opens on **the home**: a greeting with the day's state and up to seven cards, ranked for
this moment. There are no fixed sections. A meeting about to start, a reply someone is waiting for,
a call that just ended with follow-ups proposed, a suggestion with its evidence, a feature worth
trying, a routine's result, a chat to pick up again: each appears only when it is worth showing,
and a grey line says which kinds were left out. On a phone the same stream is one column, cut at
five cards.

Every button sends a prompt to the agent, in the chat in front of you, so the work stays visible in
a conversation. Nothing on the home acts on its own.

## Use it

- **Act on a card.** Press its button: *Prepare*, *Send bot*, *Review follow-ups*, *Draft*. The
  prompt goes into the new chat and the agent does it there.
- **Say no.** `✕` asks why: *wrong*, *done already* or *not now*. **Snooze** (the clock) hides a
  card for a day. A reply someone is waiting on can be pushed down by your answers but never hidden
  for good: it comes back after a day.
- **Message a person.** Hover (or tap) any person on a card: their name, role, last contact and
  whether they are on Vexa, with **Message** and **Open page**.
  - Someone **on Vexa**: Message opens the one direct chat the two of you share (it is created the
    first time). Minutes stays quiet in it unless you `@mention` it.
  - Someone **external**: Message opens an agent chat addressed to them. The agent only drafts;
    nothing is sent until you give your explicit go on the final text.
- **Ask the agent.** *"What did you suggest last week and what came of it?"* (`home_history`) and
  *"What does Vexa know about me?"* (`customer_profile`). You can correct anything it says
  (`customer_correct`); your correction wins.

## A dashboard of work in progress

Each card shows where its work has got to. A meeting moves from *prepare* to *prepared* (its prep
chat exists), *live*, *ended* and *follow-up*; a reply leaves the home once it is sent; a suggestion
you acted on shows *In progress*. When a chat already exists for an item, its first button is
**Continue** and opens that chat; a new chat starts only when nothing exists yet. Small links on a
card go to the meeting, the entity and workspace pages, and outside links it is about.

The state comes from evidence, not clicks alone: a prep chat that exists, a meeting that started,
and the agent itself, which records where the work reached with `home_item_update` when it finishes
(each prompt a card sends names its item). Open homes update in place over the live connection,
marked *updated just now*. A new ranking is applied only when the pointer is off the home, so cards
never reorder under the cursor.

People are first-class: a direct chat a colleague moved, who shared what with you, calls started in
chats you are in. Pictures are colleagues' own photos ([Your photo](/how-to/your-photo)) and company logos; a green dot means a colleague
is active now in a chat you share (anyone can turn their dot off). The home also proposes:

- **Automations**: something you started by hand three times in two weeks becomes a routine
  draft (schedule, instruction, where it reports), showing what it costs against your daily runs.
- **Connections**: a tool you mention that is not connected yet, and what connecting it would unlock.
- **How-tos** tied to your own work, each ending in *Try it*. Never more than two at once.

## Look closer without leaving

The same blocks draw on the home, in workspace pages and in agent replies, so they behave the same
everywhere:

- **Peek**: click a person or a linked page to see a summary, key details and its own blocks.
  *Open* goes there; `Esc` or `×` closes it. On a phone it is a bottom sheet.
- **Collapse**: fold any card or labelled section to one line, and open it again in place. Long
  lists show three rows and *Show more*.
- **Expand**: the ⤢ on a card asks the agent to elaborate on that card alone. The answer appears
  under it; on a page you can keep it.

In a page or a reply, blocks are a fenced block in plain markdown, so the file stays readable in
git and to an agent:

````markdown
```vexa
{"v":1,"blocks":[{"type":"title","text":"Pilot: open items"},{"type":"list","rows":[{"type":"row","text":"SSO list","meta":"due Fri"}]}]}
```
````

## Getting started is the same page

For a new person the home **is** the onboarding. The first visit shows the first steps of the
company's onboarding curriculum (connect your calendar, send the bot to a meeting, connect your
email, and later a voice note, a workspace, a shared chat, a routine, an `@mention`, a company
record). At most two steps show at a time, each only after the steps it needs. A step is done when
your **usage** shows it, never because you clicked its card. A step dismissed as wrong or done is
not offered again; a snoozed one comes back the next day. Later steps appear as *Try* cards once
you are settled.

The curriculum is a file the administrator edits: `_global/onboarding.md`, one `## <step>` per
capability with its label, why, prompt, prerequisites and `done when` evidence. The next
composition reads it.

## Lifecycle is the organizing axis

What the home is for depends on your **stage**, decided from usage by the company's playbook,
`_global/lifecycle.md`. Stages are checked from the top of the file down; each has entry criteria
(for example `meetings >= 5`), a goal, the home's **purpose mix** (work, people, automation,
connections, teaching), the onboarding steps in scope, caps on nudges and a success metric. The
seeded stages are *new* (mostly teaching and connections), *activating* (first real work), *active*
(work, plus what saves time next), *power* (automation and high-leverage moves), *drifting* and
*dormant* (what moved while you were away, and one reason to come back). When there is room the home
always shows at least one card that is not routine work, and never more than two how-tos.

Edit the playbook and the next composition follows it. Every change of stage is recorded with its
evidence, and every composition records its mix and why, so the weights can be tuned from outcomes.

Other parts of Vexa can add cards: a source registers with the home composer
(`home_sources.register_source`) and returns candidates as `blocks.v1` cards with a stable item id.

## How it works

- **Composed in the background, read on view.** A system flow, `home_compose`, composes your home
  when it goes stale: a meeting started or ended, a routine ran, a notification arrived, a meeting
  is within the hour, or the work behind a card moved (`home_item_update`); otherwise at most every
  two hours while you use Vexa. Staleness is noticed when the home is read or a card's work moves,
  so someone who does not open Vexa costs nothing. It collects candidates from what Vexa already holds — your meetings
  (upcoming and just finished), what is waiting on you (`whats_waiting`, including unanswered mail
  your routines found), your notifications, your chats and routine runs, and the onboarding
  curriculum — and ranks them. Opening a new chat **reads the stored result**; no model is called
  per view. The very first visit composes once on the spot without a model, so it is never blank.
- **One cheap model call at most.** Ranking uses a deterministic score (urgency, your stage, and
  what you acted on or dismissed before, per kind and per person). The cheapest model in the
  operator's catalog — `VEXA_HOME_MODEL`, or the first small-tier model (haiku, mini, flash, nano,
  small, lite) — may then reorder the top, mark a card wide, nest a suggestion in a card and word
  the greeting. It only orders cards it was given by id; it never writes a card. An Opus-class
  model is never used. With no eligible model the deterministic order stands.
- **Expected cost.** For an active person, about 6–10 compositions a day, so 6–10 small-model
  calls (capped at 14 a day per person; past the cap, ranking is deterministic). Nothing for
  someone who does not open Vexa. Composition runs are system flows: they **do not count against
  your routine quota**.
- **Cards are data.** A card is a small tree of primitives from the `blocks.v1` contract —
  title, text, person, row, list, evidence, checklist, action, plus group and attach. The home's
  envelope is `home-card.v1`. Both agent-api and the app drop anything else and cap all text before
  it is drawn. Expanding a card makes one call to the same small model, using only what the card
  says (at most 40 a day per person).
- **Pictures through Vexa.** A colleague's photo is the one identity keeps for them, shown only to
  people who share a workspace or chat with them ([Your photo](/how-to/your-photo)). Nobody else's
  face is looked up anywhere. Company site icons are
  fetched by agent-api through the same guarded fetcher page pictures use: no private or internal
  addresses, every redirect re-checked, images only (no SVG), at most 256 KB, cached for a week. The
  browser asks only Vexa. `external_images: off` in `_global/POLICIES.md` turns this off, and
  initials are shown instead.
- **Nothing is overwritten.** Every composition is appended, with its trigger, model, a summary
  of its inputs (source ids only) and its cards, and every card's outcome is an event: shown,
  clicked, sent into which chat, dismissed and why, snoozed. Beside each composition is
  `home-customer.v1`, what Vexa has learned about you as a customer — stage and why, capabilities
  adopted, jobs, patterns, preferences, friction, open loops, health, next-best-actions with the
  hypothesis behind each and how success is measured, and the experiment log. Each field carries
  its evidence ids, a confidence and when it changed; the next composition reads it and records
  what changed and why. It is never drawn as cards.

## Why it complies

- **Yours alone.** Compositions, events and the customer profile are rows keyed to you in
  agent-api's own tables. No route takes another person's id; nothing is shared across people.
- **Ids, not content.** The history holds ids, titles and short reasons — never mail, transcript
  or page text.
- **Retention is policy.** `home_history_retention_days` in `_global/POLICIES.md` (default
  `forever`, like transcripts) drops your rows older than that many days.
- **Background reads follow the connection policy.** The home does not read Gmail or Calendar in
  the background unless `connection_actions_unattended` allows it (default: it does not); mail
  reaches the home through what your routines already found.
- **No send without your go.** Messages to external people are drafts; a draft's commitments are
  listed and sending is your explicit act.
- **Presence stays inside shared chats.** A colleague's dot reflects only activity in chats you
  both belong to. Anyone can switch it off. On [the desktop](#the-desktop), the company's
  `presence_visibility` key decides it.
- **You can see and correct it.** Ask the agent what Vexa knows about you; a correction wins over
  anything inferred.

## The desktop

With the `desktop` feature on, the home is your **desktop**. It has four sections, in an order you
choose: **Workspaces** (every workspace you can open), **Agents** (the routines and chats you put
there), **Files** (the pages you put there) and, with `desktop_cards` on, [**Cards**](#cards).

- **A workspace tile** shows the workspace's name, whether it is private, shared or the company's, your
  role there (a read-only one says "reader"), its last change ("Valeriia changed Pricing notes"), the
  people in it (up to three faces and "+n") and its last call or a live call with **Join**. A call is
  any meeting kind: a Meet, Teams, Zoom or Jitsi meeting with a bot, or a Vexa call.
- **An agent tile** shows what the routine does, whether it is running, idle, paused or failed, its
  last and next run, and where it works. **A chat tile** shows the chat's title, when it was last
  active and where it works. Clicking either opens the chat.
- **A file tile** shows the page's title, its workspace and its last change. Clicking it opens the
  page.
- **Something you can no longer open** shows as "no longer available" with **Remove**.

### Open a workspace

Click a workspace tile (or press Enter on it) to open its README as a page. A workspace with no README
opens its file tree, which offers **Create README**. Cmd-click, Ctrl-click or middle-click opens it in a
new tab. The last call, **Join** and the faces on the tile are their own buttons and do not open the
workspace. The tile's **…** has **Open**, **Open in a new tab** and **Hide**.

### Put things on it

- **Add to desktop** is in the **…** of every chat and routine in the chat list, of the chat header,
  of a file in Files, of an open page, and of a page listed in a chat's files. Once something is on
  the desktop, the same menu says **Remove from desktop**.
- **Drag** a chat or routine from the chat list onto Agents, or a file from Files onto Files.
- **Reorder** by dragging a tile onto another tile in its section, or a section by its title. A
  section's **…** also has **Move up**, **Move down**, **Move to the top** and **Hide**; a hidden
  section comes back from the desktop's own **…**. The arrow beside a section's title collapses it.
- Every change shows a toast with **Undo**, which puts back the arrangement and what was placed.

### Ask your agent to arrange it

Ask in words: "put my agents above the workspaces", "hide files", "put my pricing notes on the
desktop", "save this as Monday review" and later "restore Monday review". In your own chat, in the
turn where you asked, the agent changes the desktop and says what changed in one line. Anywhere else
(a routine, an event, a chat other people share) it can only **suggest**: the suggestion waits above
the sections with its reason, **Apply** and **Not now**, and expires after seven days unless your
company set a shorter time. A filter the agent set shows as a chip; its **×** clears it.

### Hidden workspaces

**Hide** in a workspace tile's **…** hides that workspace for you only: it leaves the desktop, Files
and new chats, and for a shared workspace nothing changes for the other members. Your personal
workspace and company workspaces cannot be hidden. The desktop ends with **Hidden workspaces (n)**:
open it and press **Show again** on any of them ([workspace states](/core/workspaces#workspace-states-active-inactive-hidden)).

### Presence

A small dot on a colleague's face means they were active in that workspace in the last ten minutes
("now") or the last day ("today"). Whether colleagues see yours is your own switch, an account
setting that is on by default.

### Turn it on (operators)

`VEXA_FEATURES` must name `desktop` (or `all`); it is off by default, it is decided by the install
alone, and it needs [the CRM](/minutes/crm#turn-it-on-operators), which stores each person's desktop.
`desktop_cards` adds cards and needs `crm` and `desktop`. On an existing CRM database, `crm-init`
creates the desktop's tables on the next start; rows already there are untouched. The company narrows
the desktop in `_global/POLICIES.md`:

| Key | Default | What it limits |
|---|---|---|
| `presence_visibility` | `workspace_members` | Who sees presence: `workspace_members` (members of the workspace), `shared_chats` (only people who share a chat), `off` (nobody) |
| `desk_placements_max` | `50` | Things one person can place on their desktop |
| `card_proposals_pending_max` | `5` | Agent suggestions waiting for one person at a time (`0` switches suggestions off) |
| `card_proposals_per_agent_per_day` | `20` | Suggestions one agent session can make in a day (`0` switches them off) |
| `card_proposal_ttl_days` | `7` | Days an unanswered suggestion waits before it is dropped (at least 1) |

These keys only narrow: a value above the default, or one that is not a whole number, is applied as
the default, and a `presence_visibility` value that cannot be read is applied as `off`. A desktop
already above a lowered limit keeps its items and can be rearranged, but cannot grow. Changes apply
within 30 seconds.

### How it works

- **Read fresh each time.** When the desktop opens, the app asks crm-api for what you placed and how
  you arranged it, and agent-api (`POST /api/desktop/sections`) for what each thing is now. The tiles
  are not stored anywhere, and the browser keeps nothing.
- **Your arrangement is kept by crm-api, per person.** Each change, by hand or by your agent, is a
  short list of operations (order, move, place, remove, hide, show, collapse, filter, save, restore
  and section order) saved as a new numbered version. Undo restores an earlier version as a new one.
  A change made from an older version than the current one is refused, so two tabs never overwrite
  each other silently.
- **From the records that already exist.** Workspace names come from the same listing as Files, the
  last change from the workspace's history, calls from your own meetings list and the calls held in
  your chats, and an agent's status and runs from the routine engine. A paused agent is paused because
  its routine is paused.
- **Presence.** agent-api applies `presence_visibility` and then each person's own switch every time
  the desktop is drawn, and keeps each person's switch for at most 30 seconds.
- **Bounded.** One read looks at the history of at most 12 workspaces and 30 files, the chats of at
  most 40 colleagues and your 40 newest chats for calls, so the page stays fast.

### Why it complies

- **Your own state, kept by crm-api.** What you placed and how you arranged it are rows keyed to you in
  crm-api's own tables; no route names another person. Each version records who made it (you, or an
  agent on your behalf), and every desktop call is in crm-api's audit log.
- **Arranging never changes a thing.** Placing, moving or hiding a tile never creates, edits or shares
  a file, a chat or a card. Taking a card off your desktop leaves the card as it is.
- **Agents never write your desktop without you.** crm-api decides from the credential, not from what
  the agent says: only you, or your agent in a turn you typed in your own one-person chat, change it
  directly. Anything else is refused and may only suggest.
- **Nothing is composed by a model.** The desktop is drawn from existing records and costs no model
  call.
- **Only what you can already open.** A file tile resolves through the same check as opening the
  file, a chat or routine only against your own, and a call only from your own meetings list. Anything
  else shows as unavailable and says nothing about what it was. A shared workspace's roster is shown
  to its contributors and owner only, as in its sharing panel.
- **Presence is coarse and narrow-only.** It is "now" or "today", never a time, never about yourself,
  and never about someone who switched theirs off. The company can only narrow it; when settings
  cannot be read, nobody is shown as active.
- **Your sign-in only.** The sections are served to your own app; an agent is refused and reads only
  your arrangement, through crm-api.

## Cards

With the `desktop_cards` feature on (it needs `crm` and `desktop`), the desktop's last section is
**Cards**: the CRM cards you are a member of or placed there. A card is one shared record, like a
card on a board: a title, what it is about (a page, a chat, a workspace, a meeting or a record), a
stage on its type's lifecycle (`To do > Doing > Done`, or `Dropped`, by default), what is next and
who it is waiting on, a description, sub-cards, members and its activity. Everyone on a card sees the
same card; nothing is copied.

Each card shows how it is moving: its stage on a stepper, what is next and who it waits on, the latest
movement (who did what, and when), "Stalled" once nothing has moved for longer than such a card
usually goes, how many sub-cards are done, and who is on it. A field you cannot read shows as
"Request access". Open a card to see its description, sub-cards, members and full activity; move its
stage (only to a stage its type declares) and add a member there.

Make a card with **Make a card** on a chat's **…**, a page's **…** or a file's **…** in Files. It is
titled as the chat or page and is about it, and it joins that workspace's board when you can write
there. **Take off my desktop** removes it from your desktop only; the card stays for its other
members.

What waits for your word is a quiet row above the sections: **Vexa suggests: (the card) · Add · Not
now**, with why, when an agent proposed a card; **(someone) added you to (the card) · Accept · Decline**
when someone put you on a card.

### How it works

- **Cards are CRM records.** crm-api owns them, their lifecycle, members and sub-cards. The desktop
  reads them with the rest of your desk (`/crm/desk/read`), and the card's own verbs (`read`,
  `create`, `update`, `members`, `membership`, `list`, and `review` for proposals) go to crm-api
  through the app's own server with your own sign-in. The app stores nothing about a card.
- **The face is declarative.** A card's face is a short list of blocks from one vocabulary (stage,
  next, waiting, movement, sub-cards, people, and a field shown as a number, text, progress, a date,
  a checklist or a list). crm-api resolves it for you and the app checks it again: unknown blocks are
  dropped, sizes are capped, and it is plain text only.
- **Agents propose.** An agent can only propose a card or a change (`card_propose`,
  `card_update_propose`). A proposed card becomes a card when a person adds it; a stage move by an
  agent always waits for a person.

### Why it complies

- **Membership never opens what a card is about.** Being on a card lets you read the card's own
  fields; opening the page, chat or workspace it is about still needs your own access there.
- **Per-viewer faces.** Two members can see the same card with different fields: a field one of
  them cannot read shows as "Request access", never as its value.
- **Nothing lands on your desktop by itself.** Someone adding you to a card is an invite you accept
  or decline; an agent's card is a proposal you add or dismiss. Taking a card off your desktop never
  changes the card.
- **Your sign-in only.** Every card action from the app is sent with your own credential, never with
  a deployment key, and only from the app's own page.

## What needs you

With the `home2` feature on (it needs `desktop`), each workspace on the home carries a status card:
what needs you there, what is working there, how many rooms were active in the last two hours, a
call that is live now, and the last change. The Now strip at the top shows what needs you across all
of them, most pressing first.

Something needs you when:

- **a proposal is waiting for you**: a job an agent put on your short list, or a change proposed after
  a call you own (Accept, or Review);
- **you were mentioned** in a chat you can still read and have not read it yet (Open);
- **a card is waiting on you**: you are on the card and its "waiting on" names you (Open);
- **a call starts within 24 hours** (Prepare opens its note when it has one; Join when it is about to
  start);
- **a routine of yours failed** its last run (Retry).

A failed routine and a waiting proposal come first, then mentions, then cards, then calls (the
soonest first); among equals, the newest first. The same order every time you look.

### How it works

Your app asks agent-api for the attention home each time it draws it. agent-api reads each fact from
the one place that already holds it: your short list, the proposals recorded after calls, your
notifications, your meetings list, your routines' last runs, the chats you are in and each
workspace's history. The cards come from the CRM, passed in by your app. Each item carries the action
it offers as a pointer to the place that owns it, so clicking Accept, Open or Retry goes there,
never through the home. References in a README (`[[room:…]]`, `[[person:…]]`, `[[file:…]]` and the
like) are resolved the same way, one call for the whole page.

### Why it complies

- **No model, nothing stored.** The home is a set of fixed rules over data that already exists; it
  keeps no copy, so it cannot drift from the real state, and the same state always gives the same
  answer.
- **You only ever see what you can open.** Only workspaces you can open appear; an item that belongs
  anywhere else is left out rather than moved. A mention from a chat you left disappears, a card that
  names a workspace you cannot open is dropped whatever your app sends, and another person's routines
  never appear.
- **A reference you cannot see says nothing.** It resolves to "not visible", the same answer as one
  that does not exist, with no name, no title and no hint of what is inside.
- **Your app only.** Both reads are served to your own sign-in; a delegated agent is refused.

## Your workspace settings

With the `desktop` feature on, each workspace on your home carries your own settings: **pinned** to
the top, **hidden** from your home (it stays open to you), **muted**, which notifications reach you
(**all**, **mentions** or **none**), the order of its rooms, a level for its agent, and which of its
sections you collapsed. Ask in words ("mute Growth", "pin Product planning to the top", "hide Old
archive from my home") and the agent changes it and says so in one line.

### How it works

- **One record of your relation to the workspace.** Your settings live on the CRM's `member_of`
  relation between you and the workspace (`crm_member_settings`), in your own organisation's CRM.
  A workspace you never changed has no row and reads as the defaults.
- **crm-api is the only writer.** The agent uses `workspace_settings_read` and
  `workspace_settings_set`; the app uses `/crm/desk/workspaces/read` and `/crm/desk/workspaces/set`
  with your own sign-in. A change names only the keys it changes.
- **Only on your word.** The agent changes them directly only in a turn you typed in your own
  one-person chat. A routine, an event or a chat other people share is refused (`person_only`) and
  nothing changes.

### Why it complies

- **Private to you.** A read answers only your own rows; nobody else, and no workspace, sees them.
- **No access.** Settings grant nothing and reveal nothing: setting one for a workspace never opens
  it, and the answer says nothing about the workspace or its members.
- **One place, never the browser.** They are stored server-side in your CRM, not in browser storage
  and not in a second copy elsewhere, so they follow you to every device and are removed with your
  CRM data.

## The workspace page

With `home2` on, clicking a workspace's card points your home's composer at it (unless you can only
read it) and opens the **workspace page** on the right. Documents and pages always open on the right;
the centre stays your home or a conversation.

The page has a section for each kind of thing, and a section with nothing in it is not shown: **Now**
(a live call, the next one), **Needs you**, **Rooms** (with unread counts and a lock on closed ones),
**Your chats here**, **Agents and routines** (their last runs, and Run now), **Calls**, **Cards**
(a board by stage; dragging a card asks before moving it), **Files and pages** (the README first),
**People**, **Activity** (the workspace's history) and **Metrics** (when the README names a figures
file with `metrics:`, or there is a `kpis.json`). Fold any section; Expand widens the panel to two
columns. A workspace you can only read shows no actions.

### How it works

The page draws from the same read as your status cards, asked for one workspace (rooms, your chats,
routines with their run history, calls), plus the workspace's own file list and history, and the
cards on your desk. A room's unread count is what others wrote since you last read it; reading the
room yourself clears it, and an agent reading it for you does not. Which sections you folded and the
order of the rooms are your own settings (above).

### Why it complies

- **Only what you can open**, from each fact's one owner; nothing is copied for the page.
- **Every action is yours.** Run now, Move, Accept and Invite go through the owning service as you,
  and anything that reaches other people (running a routine, moving a shared card) asks first.

## Making a workspace

"+ New workspace" on the home asks for a name, what it is for, an optional site, and who can see it:
**Private** (only you) or **Shared** (people you invite, from any organisation). **Open** (anyone can
find and read it) is shown only where open workspaces are switched on.

1. The workspace is made, its README is written from what you said, and it appears on your home as a
   card your composer points at.
2. Vexa **proposes** its image: the site's logo when you gave one, otherwise a mark it designs. The
   proposal waits on the new card with a preview; **Use** makes it the workspace's image, **Decline**
   drops it.
3. For a shared workspace you can invite people straight away: Vexa drafts the invitation emails in
   your chat, with a join link, for you to send. Members appear on the card as they join.

### How it works

The app uses the existing doors: `POST /api/workspace/new` (private) or `/api/workspace/shared/new`
(shared), a page write for the README, `PUT /api/workspaces/{slug}/identity/icon` with
`propose: true` for the image (the server finds the site's logo itself; with no site the agent is
asked to design one and propose it the same way), and `POST /api/workspace/invite-link` for the
invitation link. The proposal is a "needs you" item for the workspace's owner, answered through
`POST /api/workspaces/{slug}/identity/proposal`.

### Why it complies

- **Nothing is set behind your back.** The image is only a proposal until you choose it; only the
  owner answers it, and an agent never accepts its own proposal.
